Midweek Brief · 9 September 2026
War Escalates, Oil Nears $100
USD/JPY at a Seven-Month Low — ECB Decision Tomorrow, CPI Friday
◆ Delta Box — What Changed Since the 09-06 Sunday Brief
The US-Iran conflict escalated sharply on multiple fronts at once, and oil responded accordingly. The US has now destroyed five Iranian oil tankers in total, up from three on September 5, in response to an Iranian ballistic missile attack on a US aircraft carrier. Houthi forces opened what's being described as a genuine second front, striking Saudi oil and gas facilities directly (Abha, Najran, Jazan). Iran separately targeted US forces in Jordan with 18 ballistic projectiles, intercepted. Brent touched $99 Tuesday before closing at $97.92; Goldman Sachs has warned Brent could reach $120 if the conflict extends further.
USD/JPY has fallen to a seven-month low, down 4.2% since September 1 alone — a genuinely large, fast move. The driver is a real, confirmed policy divergence: markets now price roughly 80% odds of a BoJ hike at the September 18 meeting, with 111bp of total additional tightening through end-2027, against roughly 50bp priced for the Fed over the next 16 months. A specific mechanism worth understanding, not just the headline: Japan is a 100% fossil fuel importer, and a weak yen buys less fuel precisely when global oil prices are rising — giving Japan a direct, structural incentive to support the yen right now, reportedly in continued coordination with the US.
Equities fell sharply Tuesday — Dow -1.18% (-628 points, though Amgen's -10% drop on a failed clinical trial was a meaningful single-stock driver), S&P -0.58%, Nasdaq -0.32%. The energy sector is +40% year-to-date. The 10-year Treasury yield closed at 4.805%, its highest since October 2023.
The US-Canada trade dispute escalated further: Trump banned imports of Canadian dairy, alcohol, and motorcycles, effective September 29, in direct response to Canada's 15-50% tariffs on roughly $20bn of US goods that took effect Tuesday. This is a real, tit-for-tat escalation layered on top of the labor-market divergence already driving USD/CAD debate this week.
A genuinely new geopolitical thread, not covered in prior sessions: the UK, France, Canada, and nine other countries (including Norway, Poland, Sweden, and Australia) imposed new trade restrictions on Israel over West Bank settlement expansion; Israel retaliated directly against the UK specifically, suspending trade with settlements and closing the British consulate in East Jerusalem. Separately, Germany's AfD, following a win in Saxony-Anhalt, is reportedly planning to use the state as a policy lab for national ambitions — a real, if slower-burning, political risk for EUR and DAX.
Gold fell 0.81% Tuesday despite the war escalation — the same yields-and-dollar-overpower-safe-haven-demand pattern flagged in prior sessions, not a one-off. Whether oil's approach toward $100 (and Goldman's $120 warning) eventually flips this back toward gold-supportive is an open question this week, not a settled one.
Housekeeping: no reports Friday or Sunday this week — picking back up whenever the next documents come in.

Regime

This week's dominant theme is best described as a genuine stagflationary geopolitical shock, and it's worth naming precisely rather than defaulting to a generic "risk-off" label. Strong US labor data keeps Fed-hike risk genuinely alive, while an actively escalating US-Iran conflict is disrupting oil flows through a route that has historically carried roughly one-fifth of global oil supply. Brent rose approximately 7.8% last week and WTI nearly 10%, with Tuesday's close at $97.92 after touching $99 intraday. The open question this week is whether this becomes a durable supply shock that forces the Fed to tighten into slowing growth, or whether Thursday's PPI and Friday's CPI show enough disinflation to let the Fed look through the energy spike.

The war escalated on genuinely multiple fronts simultaneously this week, not a single new development. The US has now destroyed five Iranian oil tankers total, responding to an Iranian ballistic missile attack on a US aircraft carrier. Houthi forces struck Saudi oil and gas infrastructure directly — a real, new geographic dimension to the conflict beyond the Strait of Hormuz itself. Iran separately targeted US forces in Jordan. Goldman Sachs' $120 Brent warning, conditional on the conflict extending, is a specific, credible marker worth tracking rather than an abstract worst case.

USD/JPY's move this week deserves the most careful attention of anything in this brief, both for its size and for the mechanism behind it. Down 4.2% since September 1 to a seven-month low, driven by a genuine, large policy divergence — roughly 80% odds of a BoJ hike at the September 18 meeting with 111bp of further tightening priced through end-2027, against only about 50bp priced for the Fed over the next 16 months. There's a specific transmission mechanism worth understanding here: Japan imports essentially all its fossil fuel needs, so a weak yen directly compounds the cost of this week's oil spike. That gives Japan a real, structural incentive to support the currency right now, reportedly in continued coordination with the US — not just a technical intervention story.

Central bank stances
Fed

Holds 3.50-3.75%; Thursday's PPI and Friday's CPI are the decisive inputs before the September 15-16 FOMC meeting. September hike odds have eased to roughly 52-60% from a reported ~70% two weeks ago per one source, though estimates vary. If WTI pushes through $100, the Fed's decision could become simpler regardless of Friday's CPI — a fast reaction to prevent secondary inflation effects could favor a hike even over the objection of the more dovish committee members.

ECB

Decision tomorrow, Thursday September 10; a 25bp hike remains the market's working assumption, with Lagarde and Bundesbank President Nagel both speaking today ahead of it. The real test in the decision itself is whether Lagarde's quarterly inflation projections show 2027 headline and core clearly above 2% in the base case, and whether she validates or pushes back on the market's roughly 80% priced odds of a further hike before year-end.

BoJ

Holds at 1.00%, but market pricing (~80% odds of a September 18 hike) is now the dominant BoJ story, not the current rate itself. Today's Japanese data continues to support the case: Q2 GDP was revised higher, and nominal wages rose 4.7%, the largest increase in decades.

RBA / China

RBA stays credibility-focused and hawkish-leaning at 4.35%; long AUD/USD on dips remains a preferred expression. China's August CPI (+0.8% y/y) and PPI (+3.8% y/y) both landed in line or slightly above expectations, but the source material is explicit that this is driven by base effects and rising commodity costs, not organic domestic demand — worth reading as an energy-pass-through story more than a genuine Chinese demand recovery.

✗
A cross-asset pattern worth flagging directly: the yen is strengthening while the Swiss franc is showing relative weakness, despite both being traditional low-rate safe havens. One plausible read is a genuine carry-funding rotation — with Swiss inflation near zero and SNB rates structurally low, capital that once used the yen as its cheap funding currency may be shifting toward the franc instead. Worth watching whether this pattern persists, since it would mark a real, structural change in how FX carry trades are funded, not just a one-week anomaly.
