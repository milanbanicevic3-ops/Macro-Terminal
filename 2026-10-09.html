The Fed Kept December Alive, Oil Swung on Iran — and the Dollar Went Nowhere
◆ Delta Box — What Changed Since the 10-07 Midweek Brief
The minutes kept December alive. Every member backed the September hike and most judged another increase likely appropriate by year-end. Markets price roughly 80% for December and about 20% for October. DXY tested 102.50 on Wednesday and Thursday and trades 102.12 — firm, but not breaking higher.
Both active shorts stalled just short of their first targets. EUR/USD fell to 1.1165 on Wednesday, five pips above the 1.1160 target, and trades 1.1216. NZD/USD found buyers at 0.5580–0.5586 on three separate days against a 0.5580 target. Neither stop is under threat.
Oil swung on Iran in both directions. Tanker attacks around the Gulf hit their highest weekly count since the war began and Hormuz transits fell to a two-month low; WTI jumped 3.6% on Thursday. On Friday President Trump said the US would not strike Iran before the November 3 midterms, and Tehran is reviewing a US response on reopening the Strait within seven days. WTI is down 1% to $90.59.
Gold printed a new two-month low and reversed. It hit $4,066.5 on Wednesday and trades $4,183.8, up 1.2% today, as the 10-year eased to about 5.24% after a strong 30-year auction. NAS100 fell 1.4% on Thursday from Tuesday’s record.

Regime

The dollar held its gains this week but stopped adding to them. DXY is up 0.20% after weekly gains of 0.88% and 0.98%, and it has stalled twice at 102.50. Nothing in the data broke the regime — the minutes leaned hawkish and claims stayed low — but the 10-year easing to about 5.24% after a strong 30-year auction removed the term-premium push that drove the previous two weeks.

The euro is still the weak link, but the selling has slowed. EUR/USD lost only 0.33% on the week and has held above Monday’s 1.1161 low for four sessions. EUR/GBP fell 0.30% and has not traded above 0.8493 since Monday. France has not been resolved; it has stopped getting worse in the price.

Iran is back as a two-sided weekend risk. Attacks on shipping drove the highest weekly count since the war began, then Washington signalled restraint until the midterms while Tehran reviews a Hormuz reopening proposal. A credible reopening would drop oil, hurt CAD and support the disinflation case; a breakdown would reverse all of it. That decision may come while FX is closed.

Canada’s employment report at 12:30 GMT is the main non-US event today. CAD shorts were crowded at the last count and oil is headline-driven, so the reaction can be outsized either way.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

The week’s theme was a strong dollar that stopped trending. The minutes kept a December hike near 80% priced and claims stayed at 197k, yet DXY rose only 0.20% and stalled twice at 102.50. The 10-year easing to about 5.24% removed the term-premium push behind the previous two weeks, and France stopped getting worse in the price.

The primary risk is the weekend, not today’s data. Iran’s reply on reopening Hormuz can move oil, CAD and gold before FX reopens. Michigan inflation expectations at 14:00 GMT are the last test of December pricing this week; Canada’s employment report hits a crowded CAD market at 12:30.

The EUR/USD and NZD/USD shorts remain the clearest expressions, but both have stalled a few pips short of their first targets. Buyers defending 1.1165 and 0.5580 is information, not noise. EUR/GBP stays pending from 0.8500–0.8525; the GBP squeeze and CAD fade signals wait for their closing triggers.
