Post-Jackson Hole
Warsh Confirmed Hawkish — USD/JPY Hit 160, Japan Spent ¥15.4tn Defending It
◆ Delta Box — What Changed Since the 08-28 Friday Brief
Warsh's first Jackson Hole speech was read as genuinely hawkish, and the market's own reaction is the clearest confirmation. His core message: disinflation progress over the past two years has been "modest," roughly half of the PCE basket is still running above 3%, and "we have work to do" if underlying inflation doesn't show clear, adequate progress toward 2%. He explicitly said credit and financial markets show little sign policy is currently restrictive enough, and reiterated no forward guidance — the Fed will let markets read the data themselves. Two-year yields rose on the speech, the dollar strengthened, and risk assets sold off, gold included. September hike odds jumped to roughly 55-57% from around 35% pre-speech.
USD/JPY actually traded through 160 Friday, and Japan's response was real, not rhetorical: reports put intervention spending at approximately ¥15.4 trillion over the past month. That makes any further USD/JPY upside genuinely asymmetric — multiple sources this week explicitly flag it as their highest-conviction short-on-rallies idea specifically because of how large and confirmed that intervention already is, not just a a risk still to come.
Gold and silver both fell sharply into Friday's close — gold -2.96% to $4,459.00, silver -3.99% to $66.30. Speculative gold positioning is still extreme (CFTC net longs rose again to roughly 243,300 contracts), which is exactly the setup that makes a hot payrolls report on Friday capable of triggering a further liquidation wave rather than a stabilization.
Equity positioning tells a quieter but real story underneath Friday's modest index declines: S&P 500 speculative futures positioning reportedly flipped from -10.6k to -68k contracts — genuinely defensive rather than euphoric, despite the index sitting near record levels on Nvidia's strength.
Data-quality flag, and a real one this time: one of this week's source models cited the RBA cash rate at 3.60% and the RBNZ OCR at 2.25%, both a meaningful departure from the 4.35% and 2.50% levels confirmed repeatedly across every prior session this month, with no corroborating primary-source confirmation and no reported rate decision that would explain either move. Treated as very likely erroneous rather than adopted — this brief retains 4.35% (RBA) and 2.50% (RBNZ) as the last verified figures.
The Strait of Hormuz situation is not secondary background noise, and one source's framing to that effect this week was a real miscall worth naming directly. Iran continues to keep the strait closed or materially restricted; the US Treasury imposed curbs on an Egyptian bank over alleged Iran-related activity, and the administration is pressing G20 countries over Iran-linked transactions — directly relevant to this week's Monday-Tuesday G20 finance ministers meeting.

Regime

The multi-week soft-dollar, high-gold regime this desk has tracked since early August broke this week, and it broke on a specific, identifiable catalyst rather than drifting. Warsh's Jackson Hole speech delivered exactly the outcome the "hawkish repricing" scenario named two sessions ago required: he said disinflation progress has been modest, roughly half the PCE basket still runs above 3%, and the Fed has "work to do" if underlying inflation doesn't show adequate progress toward 2%. Two-year yields rose in direct response — the specific mechanism flagged repeatedly this month as the regime-breaking signal — and DXY, gold, and risk assets all moved exactly as that mechanism would predict.

USD/JPY actually trading through 160 is the most concrete evidence of how real this shift is. Japan's reported ¥15.4 trillion in intervention spending over the past month is not a rumor or a risk still to materialize — it's a confirmed, large commitment that makes further USD/JPY upside genuinely asymmetric. This is why "short on rallies" is now the highest-conviction call across this week's source material despite carrying real execution risk given how sharply intervention can move price.

Friday's US employment report is now explicitly the fulcrum for whether this regime holds or reverses, and the distribution of outcomes is genuinely skewed given what just happened in July. Consensus sits at roughly +58,000 jobs after July's -23,000 print, with unemployment expected near 4.1%. A beat that confirms labor market resilience would reinforce everything Warsh just signaled; a miss, particularly alongside another downward revision (today's benchmark revision was itself negative, a reminder that data reliability is its own live issue this cycle), would very plausibly reverse the entire post-Jackson-Hole move.

Central bank stances
Fed

Holds 3.50-3.75%; Warsh's tone was hawkish but not a forward-guidance commitment — he explicitly reiterated the Fed will let markets interpret data rather than pre-signal a path. September hike odds near 55-57%, a real and confirmed repricing, but genuinely contingent on Friday's payrolls given how skewed the outcome distribution looks after July's miss.

ECB

Deposit rate holds at 2.25%; this desk's own source material corrected itself on this point and it's worth restating clearly: the ECB's official position is unchanged policy, not an explicit tightening bias. Market pricing has shifted toward pricing a possible hike as an expectation, which is a different thing from the ECB actually signaling one. Tuesday's Eurozone Flash CPI is the next real test.

BoJ

Policy rate confirmed at 0.75% via primary source this week, a correction worth noting against the 1.00% figure cited in several recent sessions. Tokyo core CPI rose to 1.8% in August from a downward-revised 1.7%, adding some support to the hawkish BoJ case, though the yen has barely moved on it. Monday-Tuesday's G20 finance ministers meeting, with Japan's finance minister and possibly Governor Ueda expected to attend, is the next real catalyst — watch specifically for any signal of direct talks with the US Treasury Secretary on yen support.

RBA / BoC

RBA holds 4.35% (see the data-quality flag above on a conflicting, unconfirmed 3.60% figure this week); trimmed-mean CPI at 3.6% materially exceeded expectations, giving RBA a real incentive to keep defending inflation expectations. That supports AUD relative to NZD and CAD specifically, though not necessarily against a broadly stronger USD. BoC holds 2.25%; Wednesday's decision is expected to be another hold, with guidance the real variable for whether CAD can withstand this week's broader USD repricing.

Until several of these show up together, the higher-real-yield, stronger-USD regime confirmed this week stays the operating assumption — not a single data point in isolation.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

The multi-week soft-dollar regime broke this week on a specific, confirmed catalyst: Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole tone, which showed up exactly where this desk said it would if the regime were breaking — rising two-year yields, a stronger dollar, and a real, confirmed pullback in gold. USD/JPY trading through 160 with ¥15.4 trillion in confirmed Japanese intervention spending is the clearest single piece of evidence for how real this shift is.

The primary risk and the primary opportunity are the same event: Friday's NFP. Consensus sits at roughly +58,000 after July's -23,000, a distribution genuinely skewed toward a meaningful miss, and the specific reversal signal is named directly — two-year yields and DXY declining together, EUR/USD reclaiming 1.1650, USD/JPY below 158.50, gold above $4,600.

The book stays flat, and this week is as clean a real-world case for that discipline as this desk has seen. Gold fell nearly 3% on confirmed hawkish communication with speculative longs still extreme and still growing right before the drop — exactly the kind of single-session move that turns a well-reasoned long thesis into a real, live drawdown if it had been formalized without a firm invalidation point. The structural case for gold hasn't changed; the value of having waited to formalize it has been demonstrated directly.
