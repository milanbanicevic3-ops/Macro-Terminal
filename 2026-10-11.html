CPI Week: Speculators Doubled Their Euro Shorts Into a Dollar That Stopped Trending
◆ Delta Box — What Changed Since the 10-09 Friday Brief
Friday’s data were weak on both sides of the border. Michigan sentiment fell to 46.3 against 47.8 expected, a five-month low. Canada lost 68,000 jobs in September and unemployment rose to 6.5%. USD/CAD closed up 0.22% at 1.4256 after a 1.4299 high — the crowded CAD short was rewarded, not squeezed.
Speculators nearly doubled their euro shorts. Net EUR positioning went to −99,332 from −63,256 on 40,913 new shorts. AUD shorts jumped to −98,588 (30% of open interest) and GBP stayed the most crowded at −97,611 (36.7%). Positions are as of Tuesday Oct 6.
Both active shorts closed the week in profit, short of their first targets. EUR/USD closed 1.12015, about 47 pips below the 1.1249 trigger close; the low since entry, 1.1165, sits five pips above target. NZD/USD closed 0.56122 after lows of 0.5580–0.5586.
CPI on Wednesday decides the next leg. Consensus is +0.6% m/m headline and +0.2% core. Gold closed up 1.48% at 4,194.6 and the S&P 500 rose 0.59%; WTI dipped to 90.01 on the Iran restraint headlines and closed 91.85.

Regime

The regime is still a term-premium dollar, but it has stopped trending. DXY gained 0.30% on the week after two weekly gains near 1%, and it failed twice at 102.50. The minutes kept a December hike roughly 75–80% priced and claims stayed below 200k, yet the 10-year easing to about 5.24% removed the push behind the previous fortnight.

Positioning moved further than price. In the week of the French bond rout, speculators added roughly 41,000 euro shorts and 26,000 Australian dollar shorts. Combined net shorts in EUR, GBP, AUD and CAD now total about 386,000 contracts. That book profits from a hot CPI and is exposed to a soft one — the asymmetry this week runs against the consensus trade.

Wednesday’s CPI is the binary event. Consensus is +0.6% m/m headline and +0.2% core. A core print at +0.3% or higher puts December back toward fully priced and reopens the dollar trend. A soft core alongside weak retail sales on Thursday is the first credible threat to the regime since the September hike. Fed Chair Warsh speaks on Friday and will confirm whichever way the data break.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

The dominant theme is a dollar that stopped trending while positioning kept building behind it. DXY gained only 0.30% on the week, yet speculators added about 41,000 euro and 26,000 Australian dollar shorts. Net shorts in EUR, GBP, AUD and CAD now total about 386,000 contracts. Friday’s data were soft — Michigan at 46.3 and Canada down 68,000 jobs — but the dollar still closed higher.

The primary risk is Wednesday’s CPI. A core print at +0.3% or higher restarts the dollar trend and pays the open shorts. A core at +0.1% or lower, confirmed by weak retail sales on Thursday, meets a crowded book and is the scenario most likely to move fast. Iran’s reply on Hormuz is the second risk, and it can arrive at any hour.

The EUR/USD and NZD/USD shorts remain the clearest expressions, and both are in profit. They carry the same CPI risk, so treat them as one position into Wednesday. EUR/GBP stays pending and under review. The USD/CAD crowding fade is retired after the Canadian jobs data; the GBP/USD and AUD/USD squeeze signals wait for their closing triggers.
