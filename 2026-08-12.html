Regime

This brief is being built before today's CPI print, and everything in it should be read that way — the dominant theme is still open, not resolved. Consensus sits at 3.4% YoY headline (prev 3.5%) and 2.5% YoY core (prev 2.6%). A print above roughly 3.6% headline would revive the Fed's hawkish dissent bloc and could reverse the entire post-NFP dollar-weakness regime; a print near or below consensus likely extends it. September hike odds have already ticked up to 52.5% from the ~40% low seen right after Friday's NFP, a sign positioning has been quietly de-risking into the print even without knowing the number.

Oil is the genuine wildcard sitting underneath the CPI story. WTI is back above $82 after Iran reportedly added fresh demands to the Hormuz negotiations and Trump responded sharply — a real setback, not just headline noise, and one that revives talk of renewed military action. Higher energy costs feed directly into today's inflation read, and the 10-year yield rising above 4.70% (a level hit only twice before since Trump's November 2024 win) is the bond market's own pre-CPI hedge against that risk.

Gold and silver extending to fresh highs for a fifth consecutive session, even with yields rising, is worth sitting with rather than passing over. Gold at $4,381.74–4,406.50 and silver at $65.37–65.83 are pricing something beyond just real-yield mechanics at this point — central bank demand and genuine geopolitical hedge demand both remain live explanations, and neither has faded despite the partial dollar stabilization.

Central bank stances
Fed

Still on the July 29 hold with a three-way hawkish dissent, now facing its actual test today rather than a hypothetical one. With forward guidance effectively retired, today's number has a more direct, undamped path into September pricing than it would have in a normal cycle.

RBA

Held 4.35% Tuesday, unanimous, and genuinely hawkish rather than a routine hold. Bullock confirmed the board actively discussed hiking, citing inflation still above the 2-3% target band and upside risk from the Middle East. Trimmed mean and weighted mean CPI both rose to 3.6% from 3.5% even as headline cooled to 3.9% — core stickiness is the real story, and markets are pricing roughly 50% odds of another hike by year-end.

BoJ / Yen

USD/JPY's round trip through 155.20 and back to 157.76 this week is a real tell. Bessent's comment that Governor Ueda will "do what's best" is being read as implicit pressure toward a hike, and a former BoJ official warned of further intervention episodes if the yen shows fresh weakness. With roughly 90bp of BoJ hikes priced through end-2027, further hawkish surprise room looks limited — especially with PM Takaichi opposed to hikes and holding future board-appointment leverage that could tilt the committee dovish over time.

BoE

Still on the hold with three members having voted to hike 25bp. The Committee acknowledged easing inflation pressure but flagged upside risk from renewed geopolitical tension and energy uncertainty. Markets currently price roughly 65% odds of a November hike, though that pricing predates this week's oil rebound and may need reassessing once today's CPI and Thursday's UK GDP land.

ECB / BoC

ECB holds at 2.25%, roughly 68% odds priced for a September 10 hike per this week's sources. BoC stays in an easing-leaning stance, balanced against the US rate outlook heading into today's CPI — directly relevant to the active USD/CAD long.
