Regime

The soft-dollar regime that's been building since the NFP miss two weeks ago is no longer a single-catalyst story — it's now broad-based across three consecutive data surprises. Friday's soft Retail Sales followed soft CPI and soft PPI, pushing September hike odds down to roughly 27-33%. DXY closed the week at 99.67, now testing its 200-day moving average near 99.20 for the first time in this cycle — a level with real technical weight, not just another round number.

Gold's positioning data is the clearest signal that this isn't just a dollar story anymore. The fresh CFTC data shows gold speculative net-longs at the 95th percentile, up more than 20,000 contracts in a single week — the single strongest positioning shift anywhere in this week's COT set. One desk note frames this cleanly: real yields have peaked and are now falling, inflation is still running above 2% with the Fed on hold, and that combination is the textbook "Goldilocks" setup for metals specifically.

The Strait of Hormuz situation is genuinely escalating, not just persisting as background risk. Two ADNOC-linked tankers were attacked over the weekend, traffic through the strait moved close to a standstill, and the US both threatened an indefinite naval blockade and reportedly signaled intent to designate the strait as US territory outright — a materially more aggressive framing than anything flagged in prior sessions. Oil is holding a genuine risk premium (WTI ~$82.40, Brent settled $88.52 Friday) rather than drifting.

Central bank stances
Fed

Holding 3.50-3.75%, with Wednesday's FOMC Minutes (from the July 28-29 meeting) the week's single most important release. A hawkish-minority framing has circulated this week, but it's explicitly unverified against the primary source — treat it as an open question the Minutes themselves should settle, not a confirmed fact.

ECB

Holds at 2.25% after June's hike, data-dependent with one further hike still possible if energy second-round effects persist. Given this week's oil price action, that's a live, not hypothetical, consideration heading into ECB speakers Wednesday.

BoJ

Holds at 1.00% with a gradual tightening bias; growing expectations of a September or October hike, potentially the third in nine months. As flagged repeatedly this month, the yen's reaction to hawkish BoJ signals has been muted — that disconnect remains unresolved heading into Monday's Japan Q2 GDP print.

BoE

Holds at 3.75% with a split vote and active second-round inflation watch. UK labour data Tuesday and UK CPI Wednesday are the two direct tests of that stance this week.

RBA / RBNZ / BoC

RBA holds 4.35% with a tightening bias intact; RBNZ now at 2.50% after its recent hike, with further tightening possible. BoC holds 2.25%, growth resuming but facing trade headwinds — specifically a live 50% tariff threat on Canadian products with a stated August 19 deadline, a genuine and underappreciated risk sitting directly under Monday's Canada CPI release.

Worth tracking as its own thread, not folding into the general Hormuz/rates narrative.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

Gold's positioning at the 95th percentile is the clearest sign this has become a structural theme rather than a tactical trade.

The primary risk this week is genuinely two-sided rather than a single tail event, which is why this brief leads with three scenarios instead of one. Wednesday alone carries UK CPI, the US-Canada tariff deadline, and FOMC Minutes within a single day — the highest concentration of named, high-impact catalysts in any single session this month. The Hormuz escalation this weekend adds a live geopolitical scenario that doesn't wait for a calendar date.

The book is flat, and that's the right starting point for a week this event-dense. USD/CAD's retirement looks well-timed against today's Very Strong momentum reading in the same direction the position was fighting. The clearest thing to watch isn't a specific pair so much as which of the three Week Ahead scenarios starts confirming first — Monday's Canada CPI and China data are the earliest tells.
