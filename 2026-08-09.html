Regime

The soft-dollar, risk-on regime that began with Friday's NFP miss has held through the weekend rather than fading. DXY sits in the mid-99s, off the 100.00 level it spent most of last week testing. Equities closed the week strong — the S&P's best week since April — and gold and silver both extended sharply, up roughly 6% each since Tuesday's close. Every source received this week agrees on the direction; the open question is durability, not direction.

Wednesday's US CPI print is the clean answer to that durability question. Consensus centers on +0.2% month-on-month for both headline and core. A hot print (headline above roughly 0.4%, core above roughly 0.3%, per the regime-shift threshold flagged by this week's sources) would revive the Fed's three-member hawkish dissent bloc and could reverse the entire post-NFP dollar move. A soft or in-line print extends the current regime and likely pressures DXY toward fresh multi-week lows.

The Strait of Hormuz situation remains a genuine background risk rather than a resolved one. Iran and Oman reportedly reached an arrangement to reopen the strait, but it's described as a temporary 2-4 month interim deal with no transit fees during that window, and it's unclear whether the Trump administration will sign off given it would require lifting the US naval blockade of Iranian ports. Full reopening still requires mine clearance. This is progress, not resolution.

Central bank stances
Fed

Still on the July 29 hold with a three-way hawkish dissent, now facing its first real test since Friday's soft NFP. Fed speakers this week were split — Governor Lisa Cook said she'd support a further hike if inflation doesn't soon ease, while San Francisco Fed's Mary Daly struck a more neutral tone. That division is exactly why Wednesday's CPI carries so much weight.

BoE

Still on the July 30 6-3 hawkish hold — nothing new this week. With the GBP/USD short now closed, this stance is background context rather than a live position driver, though UK Q2 GDP on August 13 is the next real test of whether the hawkish tilt is justified by the data.

BoJ / Yen

The August 1-3 joint intervention remains the dominant yen story. USD/JPY closed Friday at 157.76, essentially flat on the week despite the broad dollar weakness elsewhere — a sign the intervention floor is still doing real work against what would otherwise be a sharper JPY move.

RBA

Decision Tuesday, August 11 — a hold at 4.35% is priced. Strong Australian employment (+76.3k) supports the RBA's relatively hawkish G10 stance; guidance tone is what actually moves AUD crosses here, not the hold itself.

ECB / BoC / RBNZ

ECB holds at 2.25%, data-dependent, no fresh signal. BoC stays patient/dovish-leaning on softer domestic momentum. RBNZ's dovish bias continues to build on the unemployment spike flagged two sessions ago.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

The soft-dollar, risk-on regime triggered by Friday's NFP miss held through the weekend rather than fading, and gold and silver both extending roughly 6% since Tuesday is the clearest single confirmation of that. DXY sits in the mid-99s, equities closed their best week since April, and the raw COT data shows real, broad short-covering across every major currency except one.

That one exception is CAD, and it's the primary risk and the primary opportunity in the same data point. CAD shorts remain the most extreme and least-covered in the entire G10 set. Wednesday's CPI is the actual test of whether this regime extends (in which case that CAD positioning becomes genuine squeeze fuel) or reverses (in which case CAD's extreme short positioning suddenly looks a lot less crowded and a lot more justified).

Two of three active setups closed this week exactly as their mechanisms were built to, and the third needs a real decision rather than a fifth carry-forward. GBP/USD and NZD/USD shorts both stopped out on confirmed Friday closes.
