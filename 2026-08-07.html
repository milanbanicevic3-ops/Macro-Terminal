Regime

A genuine, confirmed labor-market miss, not a soft beat dressed up as one. The US economy shed 23,000 jobs in July against a consensus of roughly 83,000 gained — one of the larger surprises of the year. The unemployment rate's dip to 4.1% is not the good-news read it looks like on the surface: it happened because the participation rate fell to 61.4%, the lowest level in over five years, meaning people left the labor force rather than found work. June was revised down to +20,000. Job losses were concentrated in local government education (-50,000) and retail (-19,000).

Markets took the classic "good news is bad news" read. September hike odds fell to roughly 40% from roughly 55% pre-print. The 10-year Treasury yield eased 4-6bp to around 4.62%, the 2-year fell a sharper 6-8bp to around 4.17% (most sensitive to Fed expectations), and the 30-year eased 2bp to roughly 5.19%. The dollar index broke back below the 100.00 level it had been testing all week. Equities rose on the rate-relief read: the S&P 500 is on track for its best week since April, alongside gains in the Dow and a stronger Nasdaq.

Central bank stances
Fed

Held 3.50–3.75% on July 29 with a three-way hawkish dissent, and Warsh had dropped forward guidance going into today. That combination meant today's data had an outsized, undamped path into pricing — and it moved the wrong way for the hawkish faction. September hike odds falling to ~40% from ~55% is a real, live repricing, not noise.

BoE

Still sitting on the July 30 6-3 hawkish hold (three members wanted a hike to 4%) — nothing new from the Committee this week. That hawkish tilt is exactly what's been squeezing the GBP/USD short, and today's dollar-negative NFP is a second, independent tailwind for cable on top of it.

BoJ / Yen

The joint US-Japan intervention from August 1-3 is still the dominant yen story, and today's dollar weakness compounds it. USD/JPY has fallen to roughly 157.20, down from testing 159 earlier in the week — a real, confirmed move, not just a source estimate.

ECB / BoC

ECB stays cautious and data-dependent with a still-accommodative medium-term bias; BoC reads dovish-to-neutral on softer domestic momentum. Neither delivered anything new this week — both are reacting to the same broad dollar move as everyone else today.

RBA / RBNZ

RBA stays the relative hawk in G10 on resilient domestic activity; RBNZ's dovish bias is deepening after the unemployment spike to 5.6% flagged Wednesday. The RBNZ decision on August 13 is now explicitly being priced for a dovish pivot.

Hormuz — still two-sided, not resolved
Escalation, not just diplomacy
Iran reportedly struck "hostile targets" near Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz Thursday night, even while an Iran-Oman transit agreement is described as being in its final stages. This is a real reminder that the risk-on move earlier this week rested on an unsigned deal.
Oil reflects the ambiguity
WTI (~$77) and Brent (~$82) are both roughly flat to slightly lower today, tracking a combined ~9% loss for the week. That's a market that has already priced substantial de-escalation optimism and is now waiting for either confirmation or a fresh shock, not one moving cleanly in either direction.
