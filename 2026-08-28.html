Jackson Hole — Warsh Speaks Today
Nvidia Adds $442B in a Day — Global Bond Stress Builds Into 16:00 CEST Speech
◆ Delta Box — What Changed Since the 08-26 Wednesday Brief
Nvidia's earnings didn't just beat — the follow-through was extraordinary. Revenue hit a record $96.2bn (vs $92.3bn expected, +106% y/y), EPS $2.22 (vs $2.09), data-center revenue $89bn (+117% y/y), Q3 guidance of $108bn (+89% y/y) with backlog above $2 trillion. The stock initially fell on the print, then closed Wednesday's after-hours session +4-5%, and Thursday added $442bn in market cap in a single day (+8.7% to $227.98, the biggest one-day gain since April 2025) — pushing Nasdaq +1.6%, SPX to within a whisker of its record ($7,798.99, +0.72%). Supply-chain commitments jumped to $279bn, more than double quarter-on-quarter — a real demand signal, but flagged directly by financial press as a genuine balance-sheet risk worth tracking, not just a bullish headline.
Wednesday's PCE print was hot enough to matter, and the underlying consumer picture is genuinely stagflationary. Headline PCE 3.7% y/y, core 3.3% y/y (unchanged from June) — both well above target. Real personal spending was flat month-on-month (down from +0.4%), with goods spending falling nearly $50bn even as services spending rose $86bn; the savings rate rose from 2.7% to 3.0%. Weakening demand alongside sticky inflation is the uncomfortable combination the Fed has to reconcile today.
Global bond markets are under real, broad-based stress heading into today's speech. 10-year yields in France, Italy, the UK, and Japan are all rising sharply, with global government debt now above $350 trillion. The Treasury's move to double its long-end buybacks calmed US yields somewhat (10Y ~4.671%), but it's raised a genuine question about where Treasury intervention ends and Fed independence begins — a question landing directly in front of Warsh's first major speech.
Boston Fed's Susan Collins signaled a hawkish tone ahead of the speech, saying she could support a hike, not a cut, at an upcoming meeting if inflation doesn't cool as expected — raising the stakes on what Warsh says today specifically.
Tokyo core CPI came in hot this morning (1.8% y/y, core-core 2.0%), strengthening the case for a September BoJ hike — yet the yen is trading slightly weaker regardless. Japan's Finance Minister confirmed attendance at next week's G20 meeting; watch for any signal of a Bessent meeting on yen support.
Hormuz de-escalation continues but with real caveats worth naming directly. CENTCOM says mines have been cleared from international shipping lanes, though the phrasing suggests some remain; oil flow through the strait is actually rising despite the risk. A real attack on a Kuwaiti tanker (Aug 25) kept some risk premium alive, yet Brent has still fallen roughly 6% since last weekend — the market currently believes the de-escalation narrative more than the individual attack headlines. Treat each new, still-unconfirmed report with real caution regardless.

Regime

Today is genuinely the highest-risk single day of the week, and it's worth being precise about why: it's not just Warsh's speech in isolation, it's Warsh speaking into a specific, already-loaded context. Wednesday's PCE was hot enough to matter (3.7% headline, 3.3% core, both above target) while the underlying spending data was genuinely weak — a stagflationary combination that gives the Fed no easy read. Boston Fed's Collins signaled hawkish intent just ahead of the speech. Global bond markets are under real stress, with yields rising broadly across France, Italy, the UK, and Japan even as the Treasury's own buyback intervention calmed the US 10-year specifically. That's the backdrop Warsh speaks into — not a blank slate.

One piece of financial press analysis frames the stakes cleanly: the market doesn't expect Warsh to abandon a hawkish stance outright, but it wants a clearer explanation of his economic view and reassurance the Fed will hike if the data calls for it. A surprise in either direction — either genuinely dovish relative to that expectation, or explicitly more hawkish — could move all four major indices, gold, and the dollar sharply. A benchmark payrolls revision lands at the same time and carries its own political sensitivity given the recent controversy over data accuracy and the change in agency leadership.

Nvidia's follow-through this week is a genuine, separate bright spot worth not letting today's Fed-focused framing obscure. A single-day $442bn market cap addition, the largest one-day gain since April 2025, on guidance that implies 70% revenue growth in fiscal 2028, is a real demand signal for the AI buildout. The one caveat worth carrying forward: supply-chain commitments more than doubled quarter-on-quarter to $279bn, which financial press has explicitly flagged as a growing balance-sheet risk sitting underneath the bullish headline, not a reason to doubt the headline itself.

Central bank stances
Fed

Holds 3.50-3.75% under Chair Warsh; today's speech is the actual event, not a preview of one. September hike odds sit around 35-40%, December around 74-75%, both having risen on Wednesday's hot PCE ahead of any signal from Warsh himself.

BoJ

Holds at 1.00%; today's hot Tokyo core CPI (1.8% y/y, core-core 2.0%) strengthens the September hike case on paper, yet the yen is trading slightly weaker regardless. That's the same disconnect between hawkish signals and yen performance flagged repeatedly this month — still unresolved, still worth tracking rather than assuming it corrects on its own.

RBA

Holds 4.35%, hawkish lean intact and reinforced by two consecutive hot data points this week (Tuesday's CPI, Wednesday's household spending). Investment banks have turned more explicitly hawkish, now pricing a real chance of a further RBA hike within 2026.

ECB / BoC

ECB holds near 2.25-2.40%, open to further tightening if energy-driven second-round effects persist; Thursday's ECB Minutes added no major new signal. BoC stays cautious; USD/CAD remains explicitly neutral this week, with sources waiting for clear oil stabilization before re-engaging the short bias that dominated earlier in August.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

Today's Jackson Hole speech from Fed Chair Warsh lands into a genuinely loaded context, not a blank slate: hot PCE with a stagflationary consumer signal underneath it, a hawkish pre-speech warning from Boston Fed's Collins, and real global bond-market stress that the Treasury's own buyback intervention has only partially calmed. The specific, falsifiable expectation to check the actual speech against is whether Warsh gives a clearer economic view with explicit hike-readiness, not a vague hawkish-versus-dovish binary.

The primary risk today is compounded by a genuine calendar coincidence: Warsh's speech, a politically sensitive payrolls revision, and revised consumer sentiment data all land at the same 14:00 GMT moment, making it genuinely difficult to attribute the market's initial reaction to any single one of the three. The Momentum Gauge's uniform D1-strongest pattern across nearly every pair is the technical signature of a market coiled for that resolution rather than already mid-trend.

The book stays flat into today, and that's the right call given how much is genuinely unknowable about the next few hours. Nvidia's extraordinary follow-through this week is a real, separate bright spot worth keeping in view once today's Fed-driven volatility settles. USD/CAD remains explicitly neutral, waiting for clear oil stabilization rather than re-engaging a thesis that took a real hit from this week's oil crash. Gold's setup today is as clean a binary test as this desk sees all month — worth watching closely regardless of its continued commentary-only status.
