Weekly Review & Monday Brief · 5 October 2026
Week of Sep 28 + Week Ahead
The Fed Blinked on Jobs, the Euro Broke on Paris — The Dollar Rose Anyway
◆ Delta Box — What Changed Since the 09-27 Sunday Brief
The Fed hike case collapsed — the dollar did not. Core PCE slowed to 3.0% y/y, payrolls rose only 29,000 with unemployment at 4.2% and wages +0.1% m/m, and August was revised down to +133,000. October hike odds fell from roughly 70% to around 20%. Yet the 10-year ended the week near 5.28% and DXY near 101.92: term premium and fiscal supply, not the Fed, now hold yields up.
The euro became the weak link. EUR/USD fell 1.2% on the week and hit a 17-month low of 1.1161 on Monday. The French 10-year yield is near 4.9%, the highest since 2002, and the OAT–Bund spread pushed above 150bp on Friday, the widest since 2011. A contentious 2027 budget and political gridlock ahead of the 2027 election drive it; DXY trades 102.2 on euro weakness alone.
Sterling held while the euro broke. GBP/USD finished the week flat at 1.3241 and EUR/GBP fell 1.2% to 0.8471. With GBP speculative shorts at an extreme −36% of open interest, the squeeze is running through the crosses rather than through cable.
Commodities and the RBA. Gold fell 4% on Monday Sep 28 and 3.4% on the week as the heavy speculative length flagged last week started to unwind. The RBA hiked to 4.60% and left the door open, yet AUD/USD broke 0.7000 to 0.6904. WTI eased 1.4% to $91.11 after Trump rejected Iran’s Hormuz proposal and Gulf exports then recovered.

Regime

The data delivered Scenario B — soft PCE, weak payrolls — and the dollar still finished the week higher. That is the most important fact of the period. The mechanism we expected, lower hike odds leading to lower yields and a weaker dollar, broke at the second step: the 10-year traded down to 5.18% after payrolls and closed near 5.28%. Term premium and fiscal supply are holding long yields up regardless of what the Fed does in October.

Europe then supplied the dollar’s second engine. France’s 10-year yield is near 4.9% and its spread to Bunds is the widest since 2011, as the 2027 budget faces a hostile parliament and an election looms. Hotter September inflation in four euro-area economies did not help the euro — energy-driven inflation into a fiscal crisis is stagflationary, not hawkish. DXY at 102.2 is mostly a euro story: GBP/USD finished the week flat.

The regime is now term-premium plus European fragmentation. That makes it less sensitive to US data and more sensitive to Paris. The trades that work in it are short EUR against the dollar, sterling and the franc; the trades that need a hawkish Fed — long USD against everything — have lost their main support.

Central bank stances
Fed · 3.75–4.00% · next Oct 27–28

October hike odds ~20%; December is now the live meeting. Kashkari still expects one more hike this year. Wednesday’s minutes cover the September hike and will read hawkish by construction — they predate the PCE and payrolls misses.

ECB · depo 2.50% · next Oct 28–29

The question has moved from hikes to fragmentation. Lagarde urged measured hikes, and the next move is more likely in December with new projections. The market is testing whether the ECB will defend French spreads.

BoE · 3.75%

Unchanged, but sterling is now the beneficiary of Europe’s stress. Extreme speculative shorts plus a France-driven bid for non-euro European assets kept cable flat in a strong-dollar week.

BoJ · 1.25%

USD/JPY ranged 156.4–158.5 all week. Lower US hike odds and intervention risk cap the topside; the yield gap holds the floor.

RBA · 4.60%

Hiked unanimously, kept further increases on the table — and AUD fell. Speculators added 17,582 contracts of new AUD shorts in the week of the hike. A hawkish RBA is no longer enough against a strong dollar.

SNB 0.00% · RBNZ 2.75% · BoC 2.25%

CHF is absorbing euro-area haven flows: EUR/CHF fell 1.2% on the week and trades 0.9298. The SNB’s intervention threat becomes relevant if that accelerates. Canada’s jobs report on Friday is CAD’s main domestic test.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

The period’s defining fact: the US data turned soft, October hike odds fell from roughly 70% to 20%, and the dollar still rose. Long yields held near 5.28% on term premium and fiscal supply, and France took over as the dollar’s second engine. DXY at 102.2 is now as much a verdict on Paris as on Washington.

The primary risk to the book is a European policy response. An ECB backstop signal for French bonds, or a budget compromise, would hit the euro shorts hard and fast. The secondary risk is crowding: GBP shorts at an extreme, CAD and AUD shorts building quickly, and gold still heavily owned — all of it standing on a Fed story that has just weakened.

The clearest expressions are short EUR/USD from 1.1250–1.1290 and short EUR/GBP from 0.8500–0.8525, sized as one France trade, with NZD/USD short and the USD/JPY dip long carried. The GBP and CAD COT signals lean the other way and are the trades to watch if the dollar’s US-side support keeps eroding.
