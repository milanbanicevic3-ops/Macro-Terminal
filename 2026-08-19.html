Gold pulled back from its recent highs (~$4,450) to a $4,330–4,370 base, absorbed rather than a trend change — softer yields and USD softness are cited as the reason it's holding rather than falling further despite the pullback.

Regime

This week is genuinely split between two competing stories, and today's data resolved one of them without touching the other. Soft US data (payrolls, CPI, Retail Sales) has kept September Fed hike odds subdued around 30-35%, and that part of the picture is unchanged. What's new is that the US-Iran ceasefire expired Monday and the market is treating it as a real escalation — Brent near $91, the 30-year Treasury yield above 5.30% for the first time since 2007, and JGB 10-year yields at multi-decade highs. Soft-dollar and rising-yields are not usually the same regime, and right now they're both true at once.

Tonight's FOMC Minutes are the genuine swing factor, not a formality. The July 29 vote was 9-3, with three hawkish dissenters (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan), and the Committee has been reported as internally divided since. The Minutes are the first real opportunity to gauge how much support that hawkish minority actually has within the broader Committee, at a moment when soft activity data and rising energy-driven inflation risk are pulling policy in opposite directions.

Today's two CPI prints landed on opposite sides of the ledger, and that's worth sitting with rather than averaging away. UK CPI matched forecast exactly (2.9% headline, 2.6% core) — a non-event by design. Canada CPI came in hot (3.0% headline on energy costs) — a real, confirmed upside surprise. The difference matters: one is confirmation of an existing trend, the other is new information the market hadn't fully priced.

Central bank stances
Fed

Holds 3.50-3.75%, and tonight's Minutes from the divided July 29 vote are the week's central event. A hawkish tilt in the Minutes, or a front-end-led yield rise afterward, is the explicitly named condition that would break the current soft-dollar regime, per multiple sources this session.

BoE

Holds 3.75% on a split vote; today's in-line CPI (2.9%/2.6%) removes near-term pressure on that stance either way. No fresh signal, which given how loaded the rest of the calendar is, is itself a useful, boring outcome.

ECB

Holds at 2.25% with insurance-hike risk still live given persistent energy-price pressure. Eurozone final CPI lands today alongside the UK print and FOMC Minutes — a genuinely dense single day for European data.

BoJ

Holds near 1.00% with a further-hike bias intact; intervention risk remains elevated given USD/JPY's proximity to the 160 zone. Multiple sources explicitly flag outright USD/JPY longs as avoid-only this week for exactly this reason.

RBA / BoC

RBA stays restrictive and credibility-focused, supportive of AUD into Thursday's employment print. BoC stays restrictive but data-sensitive; today's hot Canada CPI plus the tariff-deadline pause both work in CAD's favor independent of the oil story.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

This is a day genuinely split between two stories rather than one clean narrative, and both are real. Soft US data keeps the Fed on hold and September hike odds subdued around 30-35%; the expired US-Iran ceasefire has pushed oil to three-week highs and long-end yields to their highest since 2007. Today's two CPI prints landed on opposite sides — UK in-line, Canada hot — which is itself informative about how uneven this global disinflation story actually is beneath the surface.

Tonight's FOMC Minutes are the primary risk, not a footnote to today's data. Checking back against Sunday's three scenarios: the Hormuz Escalation scenario is confirmed and actively playing out; the Soft Landing and Hawkish Repricing scenarios both remain genuinely open, with tonight's release the deciding factor for which one gains the upper hand into Friday's Flash PMIs.

The book stays flat, and the clearest emerging idea — short USD/CAD — is named directly rather than left implicit, without being opened unilaterally. Hot Canada CPI, the tariff-deadline pause, oil support, and now two consecutive sessions of Strong technical momentum all point the same direction. That's a real, building case. Whether it becomes a formal setup is a decision for after tonight's Minutes, not before them.
