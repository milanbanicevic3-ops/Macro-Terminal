Regime

Risk appetite is genuinely weakening the day before an uncertain Fed decision. Chip and AI-adjacent names (ASML, AMD, Nvidia, SanDisk) sold off hard, and that pressure carried overnight into the KOSPI and Nikkei. Whether this is an early sign of a real AI-sector stress point, or just a rotation out of growth names on higher oil and tightening fears, is genuinely unclear at this stage — worth watching rather than concluding either way today.

Oil swung both directions within 24 hours. A temporary pause in Middle East operations and renewed talk of indirect US-Iran contact pulled crude down to roughly $83, about 12% off Friday's peak. That reversed overnight: Iran attempted a strike on US forces in the region that was successfully intercepted, and WTI/Brent both jumped more than 3.5%, helped further by a large API-reported crude draw ahead of today's official EIA data.

A specific, credible new escalation risk emerged today, separate from the general invasion-scenario analysis covered last week. A Pakistani intelligence assessment, relayed through a German news agency, suggests Trump could authorize a limited ground operation to seize Iranian coastal territory even against his own military leadership's caution. Iran's military has publicly warned against this and says it's prepared to respond. This is a real, sourced report, not background scenario-building — treat it accordingly.

Central bank stances
Fed — today's decision

A genuinely live decision, not a formality. Markets price roughly 30–36% odds of a surprise 25bp hike today, elevated specifically because the Committee has dropped forward guidance, removing the usual pre-signaling that would rule this out. Warsh is caught between oil-driven inflation risk, an increasingly vocal hawkish wing, and open political pressure from Trump for lower rates. The most likely outcome remains a hold with one or more hawkish dissents voting for a hike — a way to signal continued restrictiveness without directly contradicting the White House.

BoE

Last meeting's 7-2 hold, with two members voting for a hike, was read as neutral at the time. Since then, June inflation slowed more than expected on lower oil, but core held flat at 2.6%, and a hawkish comment from committee member Alan Taylor warning about re-strengthening inflation risk shifted expectations. Markets now price roughly 75% odds of a September hike, with a second hike by December nearly fully priced. For sterling to extend gains, the Committee needs to signal a September hike is highly likely, either through more hawkish dissents or clearer guidance from Bailey.

BoJ

Decision Friday, hold expected at 1.00% after June's 7-1 hike. Hawkish rhetoric has picked up since (one Board member suggesting hikes every few months if the economy tracks expectations), but the market still prices only one more hike this year and the yen hasn't meaningfully benefited from the tougher talk — investors doubt the BoJ can turn much more restrictive while Takaichi keeps favoring low rates and shaping future Board appointments. Tokyo CPI and June retail sales land before Friday's decision as the nearest read on how the renewed Iran conflict and oil prices are feeding into Japanese consumer prices.

ECB

Held rates this week and described itself as well positioned to wait and watch — read by markets as slightly softer than expected. Eurozone flash Q2 GDP and a preliminary June inflation estimate land this week; a soft print on either could push ECB hike expectations out further.

RBA

Australian Q2 headline CPI slowed to 3.8–3.9%, below expectations, and AUD is the one major currency losing ground to the dollar today as a result — a two-week low. Markets still assign roughly 40% odds of a further RBA hike at the next meeting given three prior hikes and the renewed Middle East tension, so this print likely won't shift the broader picture much on its own.

Middle East, in detail
Oman-brokered talks the main hope
Significant hope is being placed on Omani efforts to broker a joint-management arrangement for the Strait of Hormuz between the US and Iran, which could end the current blockade and reopen one of the world's most important shipping lanes. Pakistan and Qatar are also relaying proposals, though Iranian leadership is reportedly demanding a permanent ceasefire and rejecting any temporary or partial truce.
The ground-invasion report
Per IISS data, Iran's armed forces number roughly 610,000 active personnel plus 350,000 reserves. Military analysts widely view a US ground invasion as extremely risky, exposing US forces to sustained missile and drone attacks without the protection Gulf-state bases normally provide. Iran's military spokesman has said forces are ready to respond to such a scenario.
