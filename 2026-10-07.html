Regime

The first test of the post-payrolls regime came on Tuesday, and the dollar passed it. DXY slipped 0.25% and EUR/USD rallied into its 1.1250–1.1290 sell zone — then the whole move reversed before London opened on Wednesday. A dollar that recovers a down day inside twelve hours, with October hike odds still under 20%, is not being held up by the Fed. It is being held up by long yields near 5.3% and by the euro’s own problem.

The US data mix has turned stagflationary at the margin. Payrolls rose 29,000 and unemployment is 4.2%, yet the ISM services prices-paid index reached 74.0 and the manufacturing equivalent 77.9. Slower hiring with faster input costs keeps a December hike alive without reviving October, and it keeps term premium in the long end. That combination supports the dollar against low-yielders and fragile credits, and it is poison for gold, which made a two-month low on Tuesday.

Oil has stopped being a macro driver for now. The G7 reserve release and Gulf exports above pre-war levels on several days pushed WTI to $86.86 on Tuesday. Lower energy removes a prop from CAD and takes some heat out of headline inflation, but it has not pulled long yields down — further evidence that the yield story is fiscal and structural rather than energy-led.

Central bank stances
Fed · 3.75–4.00% · next Oct 27–28

October is priced out; December is the live meeting. Vice Chair Jefferson and New York’s Williams have signalled no urgency, while Kashkari still expects one more hike this year. Today’s minutes describe the unanimous September hike and predate PCE, payrolls and both ISM reports.

ECB · depo 2.50% · next Oct 28–29

No backstop signal on French spreads has emerged. Inflation accelerated across the major euro-area economies in September on fuel prices, which argues for hikes; France argues against them. The Governing Council is likelier to wait for December projections than to move this month.

BoE · 3.75%

Sterling remains the relative winner inside Europe. GBP/USD reached 1.3286 on Tuesday, its best level since the payrolls report, and EUR/GBP has not traded above 0.8493 since Monday’s European open.

BoJ · 1.25%

USD/JPY is back at 158.50 with no momentum behind it. Every tick above here raises intervention odds; a hawkish read of the minutes is the route to 159 and official attention.

RBA · 4.60%

AUD/USD has been capped below 0.7000 since the hike. A firm equity tone is the only support; the currency is trading US yields, not the cash rate.

SNB 0.00% · RBNZ 2.75% · BoC 2.25%

Canada’s employment report on Friday is the week’s main non-US data risk. CAD shorts are crowded and oil has fallen, so the reaction to a surprise is likely larger than usual in either direction.

Prop firm note

Today’s risk sits in one late window. The minutes land at 18:00 GMT, after London has closed. Daily-loss limits are most often breached in exactly this slot: thin books, wide spreads, a first spike and a reversal.

An open profit is not a reason to carry full size through an event. Positions near a first target into the release deserve either a partial exit or a stop at entry, decided before 17:45, not during the spike.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

The dominant theme is a dollar that no longer needs the Fed. October hike odds sit under 20%, yet DXY recovered Tuesday’s dip within hours and EUR/USD’s bounce was sold at 1.1277. Long yields near 5.3%, service-sector price pressure at a four-year high and an unresolved French fiscal crisis are doing the work that hike pricing did in September.

The primary risk today is a surprise inside the FOMC minutes, and it is asymmetric. A hawkish text is expected and partly dated; a divided one is not, and it would land on crowded dollar longs against GBP, CAD and AUD. The standing risk for the euro shorts is unchanged: an ECB backstop signal for French bonds.

The clearest setup is the active EUR/USD short toward 1.1160 and 1.1089, with NZD/USD short carried after testing its first target. EUR/GBP remains pending from 0.8500–0.8525. USD/JPY has delivered its target and offers nothing further at 158.50 with momentum Weak everywhere and official tolerance close.
