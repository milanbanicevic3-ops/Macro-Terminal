Regime

The regime held its high but could not extend it. DXY printed 102.61 on Monday in thin holiday trade and gave back 0.35% on Tuesday. It sits flat on the week at 102.24. The term-premium dollar is intact; it is waiting for a reason to move.

Today’s CPI is that reason. Consensus has headline accelerating to 3.6% y/y from 3.4% on gasoline, with core at +0.2% m/m and 2.5% y/y. The headline is priced to be ugly; the core is the number that moves December. A core at +0.3% or higher reopens October, which is only about 22% priced.

The oil premium is rebuilding on the hawkish side of the ledger. WTI is up 4.4% this week as attacks on Hormuz shipping intensify, after Friday’s restraint headlines had pulled it toward $90. That does not show up in September CPI, but it feeds October’s and keeps the Fed from treating a soft core as the end of the cycle.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

The dominant theme is a dollar at its high waiting for permission. DXY printed 102.61 on Monday and is flat on the week. The oil premium rebuilt on intensifying Hormuz attacks, which leans hawkish, while speculators remain heavily short EUR, GBP, AUD and CAD.

The primary risk is the core CPI print at 12:30 GMT. A core at +0.3% or higher reopens October and extends the euro and kiwi shorts toward their second targets. A core at +0.1% or lower is the fast scenario: crowded shorts cover, AUD/USD closes above its trigger and the active shorts retrace toward entry. Australia’s jobs report and Thursday’s PPI and retail sales confirm or reverse whatever today delivers.

The EUR/USD and NZD/USD shorts have done their first job. Both reached their first targets this week and remain open toward 1.1089 and 0.5550. EUR/GBP stays pending and under review. The AUD/USD squeeze signal is at its trigger and needs a daily close above 0.7010; the GBP/USD squeeze needs a close above 1.3350.
