Three Central Banks in Three Days
Fed Hikes 12-0, BoE Holds 6-3, BoJ Hikes 7-2 — Yen Sells Off on the "Sell the Fact"
◆ Delta Box — The Central Bank Cluster Resolves: Fed, BoE, and BoJ All Decided This Week
The Fed delivered a genuinely hawkish hike Wednesday, and the details matter more than the headline 25bp move. The vote was unanimous, 12-0 — the first unanimous FOMC decision since May 2025 — taking the target range to 3.75-4.00%, the first hike in three years. The Committee's own dot-plot shows 16 of 18 members expecting at least one further 25bp hike by year-end, with the median fed-funds projection at 4.1% for both end-2026 and end-2027. Chair Warsh said he'd be "hard-pressed to describe broad financial conditions as restrictive" and specifically flagged that too many categories of goods and services still show inflation above 3%. One detail worth understanding on its own: the FOMC statement removed its earlier reference to "supply shocks" as the primary driver of elevated inflation — a real, substantive language change signaling the Committee now reads this as a demand-side, overheating story rather than a transitory one. Market reaction was sharp: major indices fell roughly 1%, EUR and JPY each weakened about 0.6% against the dollar initially, and gold fell more than 2%. The 2-year yield reached its highest level since July 2024, above 4.7%, while the 30-year yield fell sharply — a bullish-flattening signal that the market currently trusts the Fed's long-run inflation control even as it prices nearly three additional hikes by January 2028 against the Fed's own median of just one.
The BoE held Thursday, 6-3, with genuinely hawkish detail underneath the hold. Three members (Greene, Mann, Pill) dissented in favor of a hike to 4.00%. The Committee's own forecasts moved meaningfully hawkish: Q4 2026 inflation is now expected near 3.5%, up from 3.2% in the July Report, reaching slightly above 4% by Q1 2027; Q3 GDP growth was revised up to 0.4% from 0.1%. Separately, and substantively, the MPC voted unanimously to wind its quantitative tightening program down to zero via a new multi-year plan — unwinding the remaining gilt stock at an average pace of £46bn a year through 2034, via £20bn in annual sales alongside maturing bonds. UK CPI itself printed 3.1% for August.
The BoJ hiked to 1.25% today, a 31-year high, but the market's reaction is the real story and it's genuinely counterintuitive. The vote was 7-2, with two board members (Asada, Sato) dissenting on data grounds — core CPI still under 2% recently, conditions not yet "strong" enough to justify tightening. Two other members (Takata, Tamura) actually opposed the price-outlook language from the more hawkish side, arguing underlying inflation was already at target. That split vote, plus guidance read as less forceful than positioning implied, produced a genuine "buy the rumor, sell the fact" reaction: USD/JPY rose to roughly 157.10-157.20 after the hike was delivered, as JGB yields fell (10-year down roughly 4.9bp) and crowded yen longs were unwound. This is worth sitting with directly — a BoJ hike that weakens rather than strengthens the yen on the day it happens.
Oil has eased meaningfully from its peak, and for a real, concrete reason rather than just war fatigue. Additional crude shipments are being routed via Oman, and reports suggest roughly half of the damaged Saudi East-West pipeline capacity could return within days. Brent and WTI both fell about 1% in early Friday trading; oil now sits near $103-104 versus the $107-108 highs from earlier in the week.
Two fresh, independent threads worth tracking separately from the central-bank story. The US House passed a bipartisan measure authorizing tariffs up to 100% on countries purchasing Russian oil, reported as likely to be signed — a real escalation in the sanctions-and-trade-friction space. Separately, the US is reportedly delaying planned excess-capacity tariffs on China and other partners until after the Trump-Xi summit, with trade teams still in contact on reductions — a genuine, if temporary, de-escalation on that front specifically.

Regime

This week is best described precisely as a hawkish-but-discounted central-bank regime, not a clean directional story in either the dollar or the yen — and today's BoJ reaction is the clearest illustration of why that distinction matters. The Fed's hike was genuinely hawkish in substance: unanimous, with a removed "supply shock" framing that signals the Committee now sees this inflation as demand-driven, and a dot-plot showing 16 of 18 members expecting further tightening. The BoJ's hike today was also, on paper, hawkish — a fourth consecutive move to a 31-year high. But the vote split and the tone underneath it were read by the market as less committed than positioning implied, producing a yen sell-off rather than the rally a simple "hike equals currency strength" read would predict.

The specific mechanism behind today's USD/JPY move is worth understanding directly, not just noting as a surprise. Two board members dissented on the grounds that conditions weren't yet strong enough to justify tightening; that dovish counterweight, combined with guidance that didn't commit to a clear next step, was enough to trigger profit-taking in crowded yen-long positions built ahead of the decision. The single most important open question for JPY specifically is whether Governor Ueda's subsequent communication restores expectations for a December hike and reignites the appreciation trend, or whether today's split vote marks a genuine pause in the BoJ's own tightening path.

The market-versus-Fed divergence on the rate path is real and worth carrying forward as a specific, quantified fact rather than a vague "hawkish" label. Futures markets are now pricing close to three additional 25bp hikes by January 2028, against the Fed's own median dot-plot projection of just one. That gap is either going to close through further hawkish Fed communication and data, or it's a market that's gotten ahead of itself — and which direction it resolves is the real trade for the coming weeks, more than today's headline moves.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

A genuine central bank cluster resolved this week — a unanimous, hawkish Fed hike Wednesday, a hawkish BoE hold Thursday, and a BoJ hike today that produced the opposite of its textbook currency reaction. The common thread across all three isn't direction, it's that markets and committees are still actively negotiating how much tightening this cycle actually requires, with real, quantified daylight between what the Fed itself projects (one more hike) and what markets are pricing (nearly three by 2028).

The primary risk right now is concentrated in BoJ Governor Ueda's ongoing communication, not in any single scheduled data release. The regime-shift signal for the broader macro backdrop is now precisely specified: Brent sustainably below $95, US two-year yields and DXY falling together, EUR/USD reclaiming 1.1600, and gold closing above $4,313.67 — none currently met.

The book stays flat, and today's BoJ reaction is a clean, current example of exactly why. A genuine rate hike that weakened rather than strengthened the currency on the day it happened is precisely the kind of outcome that punishes pre-positioned conviction. Waiting for the confirmed reaction, not the headline event, remains the discipline worth holding through a week this dense with major decisions.
