Regime

A genuine risk-on cascade, not a drift. The Treasury Secretary signaled a Hormuz arrangement could be finalized "today or tomorrow," and oil responded immediately — WTI down 5.69% on the day to $75.77. Equities took the cue directly: the S&P and Dow both closed at fresh records. DXY itself is roughly flat near 99.85, still below the 100.00 level that held through most of last week, so this is a risk-asset story more than a clean dollar-weakness story.

Gold not selling off into that backdrop is the one thing worth sitting with. It rose to roughly $4,088.50 on the same day equities hit records and oil crashed — normally three gold-negative forces at once. Softer JOLTS (7.359M vs 7.40M expected) and a 5.8bp drop in the 10-year to 4.626% explain part of it, but the resilience itself is a signal the "all-clear" read on risk may be premature.

Central bank stances
Fed

Held 3.50–3.75% on July 29, 9-3, with a three-way hawkish dissent — the first three-way dissent since 2016. Warsh's language was pointed: "no tolerance for inflation," and a formal move away from forward guidance. That combination is exactly why Friday's NFP carries so much weight — the Committee has removed its own pre-signaling, so a strong print has a direct, undamped path into September pricing.

BoE

Held 3.75% on July 30, 6-3, with three members (Greene, Pill, Mann) wanting an immediate hike to 4%. That is one of the most hawkish votes among major central banks right now, and it's the direct mechanical driver behind cable's grind toward the GBP/USD short's stop level. No meeting is scheduled this Thursday — see the Key Events note below.

BoJ / Intervention

Held at 1.00% (8-1, Takata dissenting for 1.25%), but the real story is the joint US-Japan intervention run August 1–3 — the first joint action since 1998. The Treasury reportedly sold EUR, not just USD, to buy JPY, with Japan spending up to an estimated $59bn. Both sides have pledged not to hesitate on further joint intervention. USD/JPY has since rebounded to 157.660 but remains below its 200-day SMA for the first time in about a year — a genuinely new technical regime, not noise.

ECB

Hiked alongside the Fed's decision week, but into a stagnating economy. German GDP is flat, manufacturing PMIs sit below 50, and services are the only pillar holding up. That's a policy-error risk worth flagging directly: the ECB is tightening into weakness largely to avoid looking soft next to the Fed's own hawkish tilt.

RBA / RBNZ

RBA held at 4.35% and stayed the strongest G10 performer today (+0.80% vs USD) on resilient growth (~2.1%) and risk-on flows. RBNZ is a different story — Q2 unemployment rose to 5.6% against a 5.4% expectation and dairy prices are soft (GDT +0.1% vs +1.5% prior), building a real case for a cut. That divergence is the fundamental tension sitting under NZD/USD right now.

Hormuz & the risk-on trigger, in detail
What actually moved today
A specific comment from the US Treasury Secretary — a Hormuz arrangement could be finalized within a day or two — is what triggered the oil move, not a vague de-escalation narrative. Combined with the joint JPY intervention still being digested, this is a week where policy actors (Treasury, BoJ/MoF) are moving markets as directly as data is.
Why this isn't a clean all-clear
Nothing is signed. Talks have stalled and restarted multiple times over recent weeks, and gold's refusal to sell off into today's rally is the market's own hedge against that. Friday's NFP interacts directly with this: a hot print revives the Fed's hawkish faction regardless of how Hormuz resolves, while a soft print would compound today's risk-on move.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

Today is a genuine risk-on cascade triggered by a specific, sourced Hormuz comment rather than a vague de-escalation drift — oil down 5.69%, equities at fresh records, and a dollar that is roughly flat rather than driving the move. Gold refusing to sell off into that backdrop is the one data point that doesn't fit the clean narrative, and it deserves more weight than a passing mention: it's the market's own hedge against Hormuz talks that are signaled as close but not finalized.

The primary risk is Friday's NFP, and it now has two specific, conflicting data points feeding it directly. ISM Manufacturing at a 4-year high (55.6) argues for a strong print and a revived Fed hawkish faction; softer JOLTS (7.359M vs 7.40M expected) argues the opposite. Whichever way Friday breaks will move USD/CAD and GBP/USD — both already sitting at genuine decision points — more than anything between now and then.

Two of three active setups need your direct attention this week, not routine carry-forward. GBP/USD short is roughly 8 pips from its stop with the BoE's hawkish vote still working against it. USD/CAD long got real fresh evidence today (oil crash, dovish BoC, hawkish Fed dissent) that favors holding through the decision point flagged Sunday, though nothing has technically triggered yet. NZD/USD stays the cleanest hold — genuinely caught between today's risk-on tailwind and unimproved fundamentals, with its Thesis Invalidation still a real distance from triggering.
