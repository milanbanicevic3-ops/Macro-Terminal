Regime

The dollar downtrend that emerged post-FOMC has extended into the weekend rather than reversing. DXY closed Friday near 99.91, briefly piercing below the 100.00 level that's held as a floor through most of this series. Direction stays downward-biased into this week's US data, though residual USD long positioning (per COT, still building) leaves some room for a bounce if the data cooperates.

USD/JPY produced the most extreme single momentum reading of the entire series this week. Multiple sources flag suspected BoJ or Ministry of Finance intervention behind a sharp move toward the 157 area — a genuinely large yen move if confirmed, consistent with the hawkish BoJ tone from Friday's decision finally translating into price after weeks of the market doubting it would.

Middle East risk remains a persistent backdrop rather than a fresh escalation this week. Ongoing Strait of Hormuz disruption and Houthi activity keep an oil risk premium in place and continue supporting safe-haven flows into JPY, CHF, and gold, capping how cleanly risk-on the broader tape can run even with equities holding up.

Central bank stances
Fed

Held 3.50–3.75% Wednesday with a hawkish tone from Warsh, as covered in full Friday. This week's US labor data sequence, culminating in Friday's NFP, is the next real test of whether the market keeps fading that hawkishness or reprices back toward it.

BoE — from the official minutes

Held 3.75% on a 6-3 vote. Bailey, Breeden, Dhingra, Lombardelli, Ramsden, and Taylor voted to hold; Greene, Mann, and Pill voted to hike 25bp to 4%. The Committee cited Brent at $84 and UK gas at 136p/therm as of July 28, June CPI at 2.6% (with motor fuel contributing 0.6pp), and judged inflation risk skewed to the upside, with the second-round-effects question as the central point of internal disagreement.

One dissent is worth flagging directly: Catherine Mann's rationale explicitly names the collapse of the US-Iran Memorandum of Understanding as the key change driving her vote. This is the first official confirmation of something this desk had only inferred from market pricing — the interim agreement is being treated as effectively over by a sitting MPC member, not just implied by yields. Next MPC decision: September 16.

BoJ / ECB

Both delivered decisions last week and remain unchanged since. BoJ held 1.00% with a growth upgrade and hawkish forward language; ECB holds at 2.25%, data-dependent. No fresh signals from either this week.

RBA / RBNZ / SNB / BoC

RBA remains the tightest G10 rate at 4.35% with a hold-to-tighten bias. RBNZ sits at 2.50% following July's hike. SNB stays near zero. BoC holds at 2.25%, described as patient. No fresh catalysts from any of the four this week beyond scheduled data.
