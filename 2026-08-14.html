Regime

The CPI print this brief has been waiting on for two sessions landed exactly at consensus — a genuine Goldilocks outcome, not a lucky escape. Headline 3.4% YoY, core 2.5% YoY, both precisely in line. Thursday's PPI added to the soft-landing case with a flat 0.0% month-on-month headline read, the first flat print since mid-2025. September hike odds have fallen to roughly 30-35% from the 52.5% level flagged pre-release — a real, confirmed repricing rather than a scenario.

USD/JPY is the one place this week's data didn't produce the textbook reaction, and it's worth sitting with rather than glossing over. Soft US data should mean a weaker dollar against the yen; instead USD/JPY sits back above 159, having given back close to half the gains from the early-August joint intervention. A hawkish BoJ Summary of Opinions and a reported softening in PM Takaichi's opposition to a near-term hike should both be yen-supportive on paper, and neither has actually strengthened it. That disconnect between hawkish policy signals and continued yen weakness is a genuine puzzle, not a settled story.

Oil has quietly become the biggest mover of the week, and it's still unresolved heading into the weekend. WTI near $83.50 and Brent briefly above $90 (first time since July 31) reflect a real setback in the Hormuz talks — Trump added new demands Monday after Iran tried inserting its own conditions — compounded by continued attacks in both the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb, including a deadly Houthi strike on a cargo vessel. Sentiment improved modestly on Pakistani mediation reports, but nothing is settled.

Central bank stances
Fed

Passed its first real post-NFP test cleanly. In-line CPI plus soft PPI gives the Committee genuine room to hold in September without looking behind the curve, even with forward guidance effectively retired. Two Fed presidents without a 2026 vote (Collins, Goolsbee) struck a more hawkish tone earlier this week, but neither carries a vote this cycle.

BoJ / Yen

Monday's hawkish Summary of Opinions pushed September BoJ hike odds to roughly 60%, and JGB short-end yields rose to their highest in three decades. None of it has helped the yen — USD/JPY is back above 159 regardless. If US data surprises to the upside from here, the yen is described as the most vulnerable currency to a sharp drop, having already given back much of its intervention-driven strength.

RBA

Tuesday's hawkish hold at 4.35% remains the backdrop; no fresh signal this week. Core inflation stickiness (trimmed/weighted mean both at 3.6%) keeps roughly 50% odds of a further hike priced by year-end.

BoE / ECB / BoC

BoE still on hold with three hawkish dissents; UK final CPI lands Tuesday Aug 18. ECB holds at 2.25% with roughly 68% odds priced for a September 10 hike; Eurozone final CPI lands Monday Aug 17. BoC stays cautious, balanced against the softer US backdrop — directly relevant to the still-open USD/CAD long.
