The Dollar Went Flat and the Squeeze Moved to the Crosses
◆ Delta Box — What Changed Since the 10-16 Friday Brief
Speculators sold harder into the turn. In the week to Oct 13, net shorts grew in EUR (−137,573), GBP (−106,964, 39.8% of open interest), AUD (−117,299) and NZD (−47,001, 37.6%). Combined with CAD, about 504,000 contracts are short the five currencies — positioned before the CPI week reversal.
The squeeze is running everywhere except the euro. Since the Oct 13 positioning date, AUD/USD is up 0.5%, NZD/USD 1.3%, GBP/USD 0.9% and CAD 1.1% against the dollar; EUR/USD is up 0.2%. Violent protests in France are holding the euro back. DXY is flat since Friday at 101.77.
UK inflation beat and RBA language turned hawkish. UK CPI rose to 3.5% from 3.1% against 3.4% expected, with services at 3.7%, cementing a November 5 BoE hike. RBA Deputy Governor Hauser called the inflation outlook “materially worse.” Canada CPI undershot at 3.1%.
Oil spiked and reversed on a denied deal. Brent touched a five-month high above $111 on Tuesday before reports of an imminent US–Iran agreement were denied; WTI hit 101.37 and closed 97.82. Tehran says its six-day Hormuz reopening proposal remains on the table.

Regime

The dollar stopped being the trade. DXY is flat since Friday at 101.77, 1.1% below the 102.95 high of Oct 14. October is about 82–86% priced for a hold and December remains the base case for a hike; nothing in the US calendar this week can change that before the blackout begins on Saturday.

The action moved to relative central banks. UK CPI at 3.5% locks in November for the BoE. The RBA is talking openly about an outlook that has turned “materially worse.” The RBNZ’s chief economist says a late-October rise is more likely. Against them sit a Fed that will not move this month and an ECB managing a French political crisis.

Positioning is the accelerant. Speculators added shorts in every one of these currencies in the week to Oct 13. GBP shorts are now 39.8% of open interest and NZD 37.6% — the most crowded readings this cycle. The currencies with hawkish central banks and crowded shorts are the ones rallying; the euro, with a crowded short and a political crisis, is not.

NZ Q3 CPI lands at 21:45 GMT tonight, ahead of a late-October RBNZ decision where a rise is seen as more likely. Canada’s softer CPI supports a BoC hold next week, yet CAD rallied on oil and short covering.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

The dominant theme is relative central-bank divergence feeding a positioning squeeze. The dollar is flat since Friday, but sterling, the Australian and New Zealand dollars and the Canadian dollar are all higher. They share hawkish or oil-supported central banks and speculative shorts built just before the Oct 14 reversal. The euro carries the biggest absolute short and is not participating, because France is giving the market a reason to keep it.

The primary risks are NZ CPI tonight and the central-bank week from Oct 27. A soft NZ print invalidates the NZD signal on its own terms. A Fed that commits to December would revive the dollar against every idea in this book at once. Oil remains the wildcard: Tuesday showed a single denied headline can move WTI four dollars.

The clearest expressions are EUR/GBP short and USD/CAD short on breaks, with AUD/USD and NZD/USD as conditional squeeze longs. All four need a daily close to activate. The old EUR/GBP zone is retired and both earlier shorts are closed with first targets banked.
