Regime

The regime is unchanged: US activity and inflation data drive Fed pricing, 5%-plus yields pull capital into the dollar, and every other currency is priced against that. The September flash PMI — composite 58.4, input costs 66.4 — is the evidence behind it. Friday was consolidation, not reversal: the 10-year eased to about 5.17%, DXY gave back 0.21%, and equities rebounded.

This week puts the regime on trial. Headline PCE on Wednesday is expected at +0.4% m/m and 3.7% y/y, with core at +0.3%. Payrolls on Friday are expected at +100,000 after +162,000. The two releases decide whether the post-PMI repricing has gone far enough or not far enough. The combination matters more than either print: hot inflation with a soft labour market is a different trade from a clean beat or a clean miss.

Positioning has now caught up with the move. Speculators added dollar length across GBP, EUR, NZD and CAD in the week to Sep 22. That strengthens the trend while data cooperate, and raises the cost of any miss. The yen is the warning: USD/JPY dropped 1% on Friday from 159.04 as intervention speculation met still-large JPY speculative longs.

Central bank stances
Fed · 3.75–4.00%

Median dot 4.1% for end-2026 implies one more hike; officials have leaned hawkish all week. PCE and payrolls set October pricing. Political pressure for lower rates has not moved the Committee.

ECB · depo 2.50%

Improving PMIs, but less room than the Fed to tighten into weak trend growth. The euro’s better cycle has not translated into demand while US duration pays above 5%.

BoE · 3.75%

Static after the 6-3 hold; UK growth soft and mortgage-sensitive. The macro case against GBP is sound — but the positioning is now the most crowded in the majors.

BoJ · 1.25%

Gradual normalisation cannot close a 5%-plus US yield gap; the Finance Ministry is the real ceiling. Friday’s 1% drop from 159.04 shows how close to that ceiling the market is trading.

RBA 4.35% · Tue 04:30 GMT

A hike to 4.60% is priced; the guidance is the trade. Unemployment at 4.6% gives the Board cover for a one-and-done message. Explicit further-tightening language would be the AUD-positive surprise.

SNB 0.00% · RBNZ 2.75% · BoC 2.25%

None meet this week. CHF holds its defensive edge against EUR and GBP; NZD has lost speculative support; CAD trades oil and a softening domestic economy.

The yen is the opposite case — +72k contracts still to unwind supports buying USD/JPY dips, provided the Finance Ministry stays out.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

The dominant theme is a yield-led dollar that speculators have now joined. DXY closed the week at 101.03 with the 10-year near 5.17%, and positioning to Sep 22 shows fresh shorts in GBP, EUR, NZD and CAD. The trend has fundamental support from a 58.4 PMI and hawkish Fed rhetoric, and now positional support too.

The primary risk is a soft PCE on Wednesday followed by weak payrolls on Friday. That combination meets the most crowded position in the majors, GBP, and a dollar long that was added late. The secondary risk is the yen: USD/JPY’s 1% Friday drop from 159.04 shows the Finance Ministry is already shaping the market.

The clearest setups are EUR/USD short on a retest of broken support at 1.1324–1.1337, USD/CAD long from 1.4050–1.4100 and NZD/USD short on a daily close below 0.5645. They are one trade on the US yield premium and should be sized as one. GBP is the pair to watch in reverse: a daily close above 1.3350 would signal the squeeze has started.
