Regime

The dollar regime changed character this week, and the change matters for how it ends. On Wednesday the USD bid was policy-led: priced Fed hikes against falling oil. By Thursday it was yield-led: a US composite PMI of 58.4, input costs at 66.4 and a weak Treasury auction pushed the 10-year above 5.10% and the 30-year to 5.45%. The result was the cleanest test of the week — Europe posted a 41-month-high PMI and EUR/USD still fell. Relative growth no longer explains the dollar; the level of US yields does.

A yield-led dollar is stronger in the short run and more fragile in the long run. Hot data leads to more hikes priced, higher yields, portfolio inflows and a firmer dollar, and each step feeds the next. But 5%-plus long yields tighten financial conditions without further Fed action, and disorderly Treasury moves are the one route by which this trend reverses fast. Today’s durable goods and final Michigan inflation expectations are the last checks of the week on which side of that line the market sits.

Oil is back as a two-sided risk. Front-month WTI rebounded roughly $5 from Wednesday’s low as confidence in a quick Hormuz normalisation faded, and Brent returned near $102. That supports CAD at the margin and keeps an inflation tail alive heading into the weekend.

Central bank stances
Fed · 3.75–4.00%

Hawkish and getting more so: Barr, Collins and Barkin all leaned toward further tightening this week. October hike odds ~70%; December fully priced. Core PCE on Sep 30 is the next test.

ECB · depo 2.50%

Europe’s data improved — composite PMI 53.1 — but the ECB cannot match Fed pricing into structurally weak growth. The beat did not translate into EUR demand while US duration pays above 5%.

BoE · 3.75%

Static after the 6-3 hold, with UK services slowing to 51.7. Weaker relative growth and external funding needs leave GBP the most exposed major to a yield-led dollar.

BoJ · 1.25%

Gradualism cannot compete with US 10-year yields above 5%. USD/JPY’s rejection at 159.04 shows where the market fears official action; every tick toward 160 raises intervention odds non-linearly.

SNB · 0.00%

Held as expected; 2026–28 inflation forecasts nudged to 0.7%, 0.8% and 0.8%. No pushback on franc weakness versus the dollar; the Vice Chairman flagged inflation staying elevated for some time on Middle East energy risk.

RBA 4.35% · Tue Sep 29

A hike to 4.60% is ~96% priced, so the decision is not the event — the guidance is. Unemployment at a five-year high of 4.6% gives the Board a reason to frame this as the last move.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

The week’s verdict is a yield-led dollar: a 58.4 US PMI, a 10-year above 5.10% and a 30-year at 5.45% overpowered a 41-month-high European PMI, lower oil for most of the week and a hiking BoJ. DXY closed above its 100.70 validation line on Wednesday and has held above 101 since.

The primary risk is no longer a data miss; it is the bond market itself. Weak auctions and 5%-plus long yields tighten conditions on their own, and a disorderly Treasury move is the fastest route to a dollar reversal. The secondary risks are an intervention at USD/JPY 159–160 and a weekend Hormuz headline.

The trend is intact, and the entries are not there yet. H4 momentum is at exhaustion in four majors while W1 is still Weak across the board. The clearest expressions for next week are USD/CAD from 1.4050–1.4100 and EUR/USD from 1.1400–1.1450, with Core PCE on Sep 30 as the event that decides whether this impulse becomes a weekly trend.
