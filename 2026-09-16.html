The ECB delivered its second hike since the Iran war began pushing inflation, moving the deposit rate to 2.50% last Thursday. Lagarde called the move a "no brainer"; markets are pricing further hikes to come.
The oil shock genuinely deepened rather than stabilizing over the past week. A drone attack caused what's been described as catastrophic damage to Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline at a minimum of eight pumping locations, with reporting suggesting Saudi export stockpiles could run dry within 5-7 days, threatening roughly 4 million barrels per day of global supply. Brent broke through $100 Friday (closing $100.05, +$8.57 on the week) and kept climbing — Tuesday's close was $105.83, with intraday reports as high as $107-108. Houthi forces separately took control of the Bab al-Mandeb strait, a second major energy corridor, spreading the risk beyond Hormuz itself. The Strait of Hormuz is now at day 197 of closure, with commercial traffic down to single digits per day against a pre-crisis average of roughly 14-85 depending on the measure, and six P&I insurance clubs have withdrawn coverage entirely. An Iranian merchant ship was hit near Qeshm Island over the weekend, and Iran has indefinitely postponed regional strait-management talks as a result.
The bond market itself became a headline story. The 10-year Treasury yield broke above 5% on Monday — described by the Wall Street Journal as a pivotal mark for bonds — before dipping just below 5% Tuesday; the 30-year yield is at its highest level since 2004. Hedge funds now hold a record $2 trillion in Treasuries, double the level of five years ago, while pension funds have been pulling capital, adding a structural source of volatility. The 30-year mortgage rate is approaching 7% for the first time since early last year.
US August CPI came in hot on the core reading (+0.3% m/m), pushing FOMC hike odds to roughly 92-93% heading into today's decision — what would be the first Fed hike since July 2023. Per Deutsche Bank strategists, a hold at this point would represent the largest dovish surprise at a scheduled FOMC meeting ever recorded. Fed Chair Warsh, appointed by Trump on the expectation of a softer policy stance, now faces hiking directly against the president's stated preference, driven by inflation from tariffs, energy, and AI-capex demand.
GBP fell to a five-week low (~1.3486) after August UK payrolls fell 26,000, far worse than the -5,000 expected. The Bank of England decides tomorrow (Thursday, 13:00 CEST); the prior vote was 6-3 to hold at 3.75%, and this weaker jobs data strengthens the case for another hold. Today's UK CPI print, landing before today's FOMC decision, is the immediate test.
Gold's relationship with the war has been genuinely volatile this week, not settled in either direction. It rallied to $4,310-4,315 into the weekend as safe-haven demand briefly outweighed yields, then fell back to $4,291.60 Tuesday as the stronger dollar and rising yields reasserted themselves. This back-and-forth is itself the story — treat any single day's move as inconclusive given how unstable this correlation has been across the past month.
The AI sector had a genuinely turbulent week, independent of the macro story. Anthropic and OpenAI reportedly discussed slowing the pace of AI development; SoftBank fell as much as 13% after Altman said OpenAI won't IPO this year; Asian chip stocks (SK Hynix, Samsung) fell sharply on renewed capex concerns, with semiconductors down roughly 5% Monday. Despite weekend warnings from AI leaders themselves (Amodei, Altman, Musk, Hassabis) about the risks of rapid development, Monday still saw Microsoft, Alphabet, and Meta rally roughly 2% each — a genuinely mixed signal worth holding as such rather than resolving into a single narrative.

Regime

Today would mark the first Federal Reserve rate hike since July 2023, and the market is treating it as close to a certainty — 92-93% priced. Per one analyst framing worth carrying forward directly: a hold at this point would represent the largest dovish surprise at a scheduled FOMC meeting ever recorded. That reframes today's real risk. The hike itself is largely already in the price; the genuine uncertainty sits in the dot-plot and in Chair Warsh's tone at the 20:30 press conference — specifically, whether the Committee's own projections show one further hike by year-end (as June's dot-plot signaled) or two, which the market is now leaning toward pricing.

There's a real irony worth naming plainly: Warsh was appointed by Trump on the expectation of a softer policy path, and today he is very likely about to hike directly against the president's stated preference. The driver isn't a change in Warsh's own instincts so much as the inflation backdrop itself — tariffs, energy costs from the oil shock, and AI-capex-driven demand have combined to make August's core CPI print hot enough (+0.3% m/m) that the Committee's own credibility now depends on responding to it.

The oil and bond stories underneath today's decision are not backdrop — they are direct inputs to it, and both have gotten more serious over the past week, not less. Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline remains damaged after a drone attack, with reporting suggesting export stockpiles could run dry within days; Brent has pushed toward $108. The 10-year Treasury yield crossing 5% on Monday, described directly as a pivotal mark for the bond market, adds a second, independent source of tightening financial conditions that the Fed has to weigh alongside its own rate decision.

Today's UK CPI print, landing before this evening's FOMC decision, is the immediate test of whether inflation gives the hawkish minority any fresh ammunition.

BoJ

USD/JPY has stabilized near 155.25 after its earlier sharp collapse from above 160, though reflexivity remains high and intervention risk stays elevated on any renewed move. The pair's next major catalyst is BoJ's own policy communications, still pending as of this writing.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

Tonight's FOMC decision, priced at 92-93% for what would be the first hike since July 2023, caps a week that saw the oil shock deepen (Saudi pipeline damage, Bab al-Mandeb lost to Houthi control, Brent toward $108), the bond market itself become a headline story (10-year yield crossing 5%), and the single largest one-week COT positioning swing this desk has recorded — JPY flipping from a heavily crowded short to outright net long in seven days. The primary risk tonight and tomorrow is genuinely compounding rather than singular: UK CPI this morning, the Fed decision and dot-plot tonight, and the BoE's own decision tomorrow all land within roughly 30 hours of each other, with GBP carrying the densest exposure of any currency to that sequence. The regime-shift signal for the broader macro backdrop remains unchanged: Brent below $95, two-year yields rolling over, DXY breaking lower, EUR/USD above 1.1650, gold above $4,500 — none currently met.

The book stays flat heading into the highest-risk night of the cycle so far, and that's the right call regardless of how strong any individual thesis looks on paper. Six of eight tracked pairs show broad H4 momentum expansion simultaneously — the market is coiled for a real move, and there's no advantage to guessing its direction before the dot-plot and press conference actually happen.
