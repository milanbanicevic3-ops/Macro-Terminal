What the yields say

The real 10-year fell 16bp — a strong move — and gold fell 3.4% anyway. Gold normally trades inverse to real yields. This week it sold off with them. That is liquidation into an AI-equity drawdown and an oil-inflation scare, not a rates trade, and it means gold is not a signal for the dollar right now.

The spreads explain the FX ranking. US–DE 2Y is flat at 164.7bp, and EUR/USD is flat. US–GB 2Y moved 5.9bp in sterling’s favour, and GBP/USD rose. US–JP 10Y widened 4.5bp, yet USD/JPY fell 0.9%: the yen is trading BoJ hike expectations and risk-off, not the spread.

The gilt front end did not react to a hot CPI and an eight-month PMI high. UK 2-year yields are flat at 4.91%. The November BoE hike is already priced; sterling’s further upside depends on the BoE signalling more than one move.

Regime

The relative-hawks regime held and widened. The UK printed hot inflation and its strongest PMI in eight months; the euro area printed its weakest in four, with France contracting. Australia and New Zealand keep hawkish central banks despite a softer NZ CPI. The ranking of currencies this week followed the ranking of central banks almost exactly.

The US crossed half of the regime-shift line, and the dollar ignored it. The 10-year at 4.982% is below 5.00% for the first time in a month. DXY is flat on the week at 101.76 because claims at 194k and a December hike priced around 80–90% still anchor the front end. A 10-year below 5% with an unmoved dollar is a warning, not a shift.

Next week is the test. The Fed, BoC and RBNZ decide on Oct 28, the ECB on Oct 29 and the BoJ on Oct 30, with US Q3 GDP in between. Every open idea in this book depends on at least one of those meetings.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

The dominant theme is a widening gap between central banks, while the dollar stands still. UK data beat and euro-area data missed, so EUR/GBP made a 17-month low. Australian and New Zealand rates stayed hawkish. The US 10-year broke below 5.00% and DXY did not move, because a December hike is still priced and claims are low.

The primary risk is a Fed that commits to December on Oct 28. That would push the 10-year back above 5% and hit three of the four ideas together. The RBNZ on the same day decides the NZD signal on its own terms. Over the weekend, strikes on Saudi energy infrastructure keep oil above $100 and keep the gap risk two-sided.

EUR/GBP is the cleanest idea in the book, because it does not depend on the Fed. AUD/USD is active and holding above 0.7000. NZD/USD is active but half-invalidated. EUR/GBP and USD/CAD have triggered without filling, and whether to take them at market is the decision for Monday.
