Regime-Shift Week
War Escalates, Bond Yields Hit Multi-Decade Highs, Gold Decouples and Falls
◆ Delta Box — What Changed Since the 08-30 Sunday Brief (No Monday/Tuesday Session Published)
The US-Iran conflict escalated seriously rather than continuing its slow-burn pattern. The US struck Iranian military targets (air defense systems, radar, naval assets, mines, communications) in response to an Iranian attempt to mine the Strait of Hormuz; Iran retaliated with missiles and drones across the Gulf, with Jordan reporting the interception of 13 ballistic projectiles in its airspace and Kuwait activating air defenses against hostile projectiles. The IRGC has threatened "heavier, broader, and more devastating" responses. The war has now run six months with no resolution in sight.
The Hormuz picture is genuinely contradictory right now, and worth naming as such rather than picking the cleaner narrative. One tracking source describes the strait as effectively closed to commercial traffic (day 184, roughly 4% of normal flow, war-risk insurance now 40 times normal rates), while the US Energy Secretary said a record 17 million barrels transited the strait on Monday, the highest since the conflict began. Treat every individual report with real caution until it's independently corroborated — the situation on the ground is described as chaotic and disputed.
Oil has surged hard on this: Brent ~$92-94, WTI ~$90 (+4.2% Tuesday), up roughly 6% in two days and roughly 56% year-to-date. This is now a real, confirmed inflation-risk input layered directly on top of the already-hawkish Fed repricing from last week.
The global bond rout has deepened materially. The US 10-year yield reached 4.795%, its highest since January 2025; the Japanese 10-year JGB touched 3% for the first time since 1996; the UK 30-year gilt hit a multi-decade high; German and French yields reached their highest levels since 2011 and 2008 respectively. Drivers cited: massive global government debt issuance layered on top of oil-driven war inflation — a genuinely global, not US-specific, phenomenon.
Gold collapsed 2.3-2.7% to roughly $4,329-4,342 Tuesday, breaking its 200-day moving average, despite the war escalation — a real, confirmed decoupling from the standard "war pushes gold up" pattern. Yields and the dollar are currently overpowering safe-haven demand. This is explicitly flagged across multiple sources as a regime change worth tracking closely rather than assuming the old correlation reasserts itself automatically.
Fed rate-hike odds for the September 15-16 meeting have jumped further, now cited between 55% and 71% depending on source, with most converging near 68%, up from roughly 30-40% before Warsh's Jackson Hole speech. Fed Governor Barr added to the hawkish chorus Tuesday, saying the Fed should hike this month unless new data shows weakening pressures. This is a genuine narrative reversal from "cut" to "hike" inside of two weeks.
Eurozone inflation jumped to 3.3% in August, a third consecutive monthly rise, cementing expectations for an ECB hike at next week's September 10 meeting. Equities fell broadly Tuesday (Dow -0.79%, S&P -0.71%, Nasdaq -1.03%, DAX -1.10%, STOXX600 -0.77%) on the combined weight of oil, yields, and the war. The G20 finance ministers meeting concluded Tuesday amid this selloff, with reported friction over tariffs.

Regime

This week is not a continuation of last week's post-Warsh repricing — it's a genuine escalation on top of it, and the combination is what makes it dangerous rather than either factor alone. The US-Iran war moved from a six-month slow burn into real, direct military exchanges: US strikes on Iranian military infrastructure, Iranian missile and drone retaliation across the Gulf, interceptions reported in Jordan and Kuwait. Layered on top of that is a global bond rout that is not a US-specific story — Japanese, UK, German, and French long yields are all at multi-year or multi-decade highs simultaneously, reflecting a shared concern about government debt issuance at a moment when war-driven inflation is also rising.

Gold falling nearly 3% into this backdrop is the single most important data point in this brief, and it's worth sitting with rather than treating as routine. War normally pushes gold higher through safe-haven demand; this week, rising yields and a stronger dollar have overpowered that mechanism entirely, breaking the metal's 200-day moving average in the process. Multiple sources this week explicitly flag this as a genuine correlation breakdown worth monitoring closely — not a reason to assume gold stays weak, but a real signal that the usual playbook for interpreting geopolitical risk through gold isn't working normally right now.

The Hormuz situation itself is genuinely contradictory, and that contradiction is informative rather than just noise. One tracker describes the strait as effectively closed (roughly 4% of normal commercial flow, war-risk insurance 40 times normal), while US officials cite a record 17 million barrels transiting Monday. Both can't be fully true, and the honest read is that the situation on the ground is chaotic enough that neither source should be trusted in isolation — treat each new individual report with real caution rather than anchoring to whichever framing arrived first.

Central bank stances
Fed

Holds 3.50-3.75%; September hike odds now cited between 55% and 71% depending on source, most converging near 68%, up sharply from roughly 30-40% before Warsh's speech. Governor Barr added a second hawkish voice Tuesday, saying the Fed should hike this month absent new data showing weakening pressure. Friday's NFP (consensus roughly +58,000 after July's -23,000) is now explicitly the swing factor for whether this narrative holds into the September 15-16 meeting.

ECB

Holds 2.25% deposit rate; August Eurozone inflation rose to 3.3%, a third consecutive monthly increase, cementing expectations for a hike at next week's September 10 meeting. This is a genuine, confirmed acceleration, not just market pricing getting ahead of the data.

BoJ

Holds near 1.00% (0.75% per one primary-source correction flagged last session); Japanese 10-year JGB yields touched 3% for the first time since 1996, adding real domestic pressure on top of the yen-intervention story. Japan's confirmed ¥15.4 trillion in intervention spending over the past month remains the dominant fact for USD/JPY specifically.

RBNZ / BoC

RBNZ delivered the fully-priced hike to 2.75% but paired it with a notably dovish tone, capping the NZD reaction rather than extending it — a real "buy the rumor, sell the fact" dynamic worth noting. BoC's decision lands today (15:45 CEST / 13:45 GMT), with a hold at 2.25% widely expected; the actual risk is the tariff-related language in the statement and press conference, not the rate itself.
