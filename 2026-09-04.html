NFP Day — The Fulcrum
Yen Surges on Fresh Intervention + Hawkish BoJ, Fed Hike Odds Cool to ~50-59%
◆ Delta Box — What Changed Since the 09-02 Wednesday Brief (No Thursday Session Published)
USD/JPY has moved dramatically since Tuesday — from trading through 160 to roughly 156.15 this morning, a genuinely large reversal. The driver is confirmed, not speculative: a fresh round of joint US-Japan intervention aimed at strengthening the yen, combined with unusually hawkish BoJ commentary. Governor Ueda and Deputy Governor Himino both left the door open to a September hike; board member Hajime Takata, known as a hawk, suggested a hike larger than 25bp or two consecutive hikes were both possible. September BoJ hike odds are now priced near 77%, with roughly 100bp of further tightening expected after that.
Fed hike odds for September have cooled meaningfully from the ~68-71% cited earlier this week, now sitting nearer 50-59% depending on source. NY Fed President Williams pushed back directly against the market's hawkish pricing, attributing the bond-yield spike to underlying economic strength rather than distress, and remains one of the more dovish FOMC voices. Fed Governor Waller separately signaled support for a September hold if inflation continues easing. This is a real, two-sided repricing landing directly ahead of today's NFP, not a one-directional continuation of Tuesday's move.
A significant, if unconfirmed, geopolitical headline: Trump has reportedly told advisors privately he favors declaring the six-month Iran war over, betting that sustained economic pressure forces Iran to abandon its nuclear program or face regime collapse. Real, concrete signs point the same direction — the US military escorted 40 tankers carrying 18 million barrels through Hormuz Tuesday, a wartime maximum near pre-war levels. But the conflict is not resolved on the ground: Hormuz remains effectively closed at roughly 7% of normal commercial traffic after 185 days, war-risk insurance sits 40 times normal, and fresh reports this morning describe Iranian missile and drone attacks on a Kuwaiti military base. Defense Secretary Hegseth has extended US troop deployment in the region through 2027 as a hedge. Treat the "war may be ending" framing as one real, plausible thread among several, not a settled outcome.
Gold has recovered from Tuesday's anomalous 2.3-2.7% drop, closing Thursday near $4,519 — most of that recovery tracks directly with the dollar softening and yields easing on Williams' comments, not a change in the war-correlation story itself. Whether this fully resolves the "gold decoupled from the war" question flagged Wednesday, or was simply a rates-driven bounce, is still an open question this desk isn't ready to call either way.
The global bond rout context sharpened with specific, striking figures: French 10-year yields at 4.25% (+0.69pp this year), UK 30-year gilts at 5.16%, Japanese 30-year yields hitting a record 4.2% Wednesday, and total US gross government debt crossing $40 trillion for the first time in August. Equities recovered part of Tuesday's losses Wednesday and Thursday (Dow +0.56%, Nasdaq +0.5% Wednesday; SPX closed Thursday near its 7,747.71 record-adjacent level).
Today's NFP (consensus ~+56,000 jobs, 4.1% unemployment) is exactly the decisive event this desk has flagged for two straight weeks — still pending as this brief is built. Everything above should be read as the pre-print setup, not a forecast of the outcome.

Regime

The hawkish, one-directional momentum from Warsh's Jackson Hole speech has met real, credible pushback this week, and it's worth being precise about the source of that pushback rather than treating it as vague market noise. NY Fed President Williams directly challenged the market's ~70% September hike pricing, attributing the bond-yield spike to economic strength rather than distress — a specific, on-the-record dovish counterweight from a sitting FOMC voter. Fed Governor Waller separately signaled openness to a hold if inflation continues easing. Combined, September hike odds have eased from the 68-71% range to roughly 50-59% ahead of today's print.

USD/JPY's move this week is the clearest, most concrete expression of that shift, and it's driven by confirmed action rather than speculation. A fresh round of joint US-Japan intervention, layered on top of unusually direct hawkish signaling from BoJ leadership — Governor Ueda, Deputy Governor Himino, and hawk board member Takata all pointing toward a September hike, with Takata specifically floating a larger-than-25bp move or two consecutive hikes — has pushed the pair from above 160 to roughly 156 in a matter of days. September BoJ hike odds near 77% now sit well above the Fed's own ~50-59%, a genuine, rare monetary-policy divergence working in the yen's favor rather than the dollar's.

The Iran war headline this week deserves care in how it's framed, since the two pieces of information point in different directions. Reports that Trump privately favors declaring the war over are real and specific (WSJ-sourced), and the US escorting a wartime-maximum 40 tankers through Hormuz Tuesday is a concrete, verifiable fact consistent with that framing. But the conflict has not stopped: Hormuz remains at roughly 7% of normal commercial traffic, war-risk insurance sits 40 times normal, and fresh Iranian missile and drone attacks on a Kuwaiti military base were reported this morning. Both threads are true simultaneously — a private preference to end the war is not the same as the war actually ending.

Central bank stances
Fed

Holds 3.50-3.75%; today's NFP is the final major labor input before the September 15-16 meeting, and it's genuinely decisive given how split the Committee's own public commentary has been this week (Williams dovish-leaning, Waller open to a hold, versus Barr's hawkish tone flagged Tuesday). A print materially below the ~56,000 consensus, especially with rising unemployment or cooling wage growth, would very plausibly tip the balance toward the dovish voices; a strong beat would do the opposite.

BoJ

The most consequential central bank story of the week, not a secondary one. September hike odds near 77%, with roughly 100bp of further tightening priced after that — a real, confirmed, and now dollar-negative divergence from the Fed's own more uncertain path. Japan's fresh intervention alongside this hawkish repricing is a coordinated, two-pronged effort, not a single lever.

RBA / BoC

RBA stays credibility-focused and hawkish-leaning; AUD/USD long-on-dips remains a preferred expression given Australia's relative rate support versus a more uncertain Fed path. BoC held at 2.25% as expected earlier this week; today's Canadian employment data lands alongside US NFP, adding a second, direct CAD catalyst on the same release.

Global Bond Markets

This remains a genuinely global fiscal story, not a US-specific one. French 10-year yields at 4.25% (+0.69pp this year), UK 30-year gilts at 5.16%, Japanese 30-year yields at a record 4.2%, and total US gross government debt crossing $40 trillion for the first time in August. One piece of financial press analysis this week described this directly as the bond market handing world leaders a failing grade on fiscal discipline — a framing worth keeping in view independent of any single central bank's next move.
