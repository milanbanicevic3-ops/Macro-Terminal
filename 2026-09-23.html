Regime

The dollar and oil have decoupled, and that settles Sunday’s central question: the USD bid is policy-led. Crude fell through $90 on the WTI front month and Brent slipped below $100 as the US and Iran held a three-hour meeting in Qatar and Saudi Arabia restarted its East-West pipeline. The textbook reaction would be lower inflation expectations, lower yields and a softer dollar. Instead DXY climbed to 100.77 and EUR/USD fell to its lowest since July. The market is pricing the Fed path, not the oil tape.

Rate pricing explains the bid. Fed funds futures carry roughly 58% odds of a second consecutive hike in October, a December move is more than fully priced, and about 80bp of further tightening is priced through end-2027. The Committee’s own median sits at 4.1% for end-2026. With that much priced, the dollar needs data to keep validating it — which makes today’s US flash PMI at 13:45 GMT the pivot of the week, with the price components as important as the headline.

Oil is now a CAD story, not a USD story. Lower crude removes Canada’s terms-of-trade support while the US rate premium stays intact, which is why USD/CAD has broken higher. The diplomacy is two-sided: the same day Iran’s foreign minister met US envoys, President Trump used the UN General Assembly to demand a deal or face “annihilation”. A reported diesel-export ban under consideration in Washington adds a separate, headline-driven energy risk.

Central bank stances
Fed · 3.75–4.00%

Hawkish hold after the unanimous September hike; median dot 4.1% for end-2026. Barr (14:05 GMT) and Goolsbee (16:00 GMT) speak today. Political pressure for much lower rates is loud, but the Committee — not the Chair alone — sets the path, and the vote was 12-0.

ECB · depo 2.50%

Restrictive after the September hike; money markets price three more 25bp moves through next year. Eurozone consumer confidence fell to −16.5 from −15.5, ending a four-month improvement. EUR needs strong PMIs — including selling-price components — to justify that pricing.

BoE · 3.75%

Held 6-3, with three votes for a hike; labour-market softness keeps the balance delicate. UK flash PMI at 08:30 GMT — watch employment and price sub-indices, not only the headline. Speculative GBP shorts remain the most crowded in the majors.

BoJ · 1.25%

Hiked 7-2, but the yen weakened: both dissenters lean dovish, and two hawkish board terms expire next summer. Market pricing of roughly 100bp of BoJ hikes through 2027 looks demanding. USD/JPY above 157.50 sits in the zone where rate-check headlines have surfaced before.

SNB · 0.00% · Thu

Hold expected; the franc’s reaction depends on the inflation forecast, not the rate. August CPI was 0.8% y/y, a near-two-year high but inside the 0–2% band. An upward forecast revision pulls forward a hike priced for March and supports CHF; retained intervention language caps it.

RBA 4.35% · RBNZ 2.75% · BoC 2.25%

An RBA hike on Sep 29 is priced at roughly 80%, and Thursday’s employment report decides whether that holds. The soft flash PMI is the first crack in that pricing. RBNZ and BoC are secondary this week; CAD trades oil.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

The dollar answered Sunday’s question: with oil below $90 and DXY at a two-month high, the USD bid is running on Fed pricing, not on the energy shock. That makes the regime more durable than an oil-driven one, but also more data-dependent. Roughly 58% odds of an October hike are already in the price, so today’s US flash PMI either extends the trend or starts the unwind.

The primary risk is a US services miss paired with resilient European and UK prints. That outcome hits the consensus book where it is most crowded — GBP shorts — and meets extreme speculative yen longs in USD/JPY. A collapse of the Qatar talks is the secondary risk, and it would help CAD and gold, not the dollar.

The clearest expressions are USD/CAD on pullbacks toward 1.4000–1.4050 and EUR/USD short on rallies back into 1.1450–1.1530. Momentum confirms both, but both have run: the edge today is in patience through the 13:45 GMT print, not in selling lows or buying highs. The book stays flat into the data.
