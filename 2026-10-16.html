Week Close
Hot CPI, Hot PPI, Strong Retail Sales — and the Dollar Fell Anyway
◆ Delta Box — What Changed Since the 10-14 Midweek Brief
Every US print ran hot and the dollar still fell. CPI rose 0.6% m/m and 3.7% y/y, with core at +0.3% and 2.6% — both above consensus. PPI jumped 1.3% m/m and retail sales rose 0.8% against 0.3% expected. DXY made an 18-month high of 102.95 on Wednesday and trades 101.67, down 0.55% on the week. The 10-year is near 5.09%, a three-week low.
Fed officials talked October down. Daly said further hikes are more likely than not but signalled no need to move this month; Bowman urged waiting; Warsh declined to pre-commit, calling October and December “crystallising moments.” October hike odds fell from about 50% after CPI to roughly 27–34%.
Both active shorts are closed. EUR/USD fell to 1.1113 on Wednesday, 24 pips short of the second target, then reversed through entry; the remainder was stopped at breakeven after the first target. NZD/USD cleared its first target at 0.5565, then rallied to 0.5670 and was closed ahead of its technical invalidation as the dollar leg of the thesis reversed.
Both COT signals are off the table. The GBP/USD squeeze signal is void: its named invalidation, core CPI at +0.3%, printed on Wednesday, even though GBP/USD now trades 1.3334. The AUD/USD signal never triggered — 0.7013 intraday, a 0.6973 close — and weak Australian jobs data sent AUD to 0.6894.

Regime

The data confirmed the regime and the market sold it. Core CPI beat, PPI posted its largest monthly rise since March 2022 and retail sales more than doubled consensus. On the regime’s own logic that should have sent the dollar through 103. Instead DXY reversed from 102.95 and is heading for its first weekly fall in five.

The Fed, not the data, set the price. Daly, Bowman and Warsh all declined to endorse an October move after the hot prints. Markets read that as a Fed that will tolerate hot inflation rather than chase it, and the 10-year fell from above 5.30% to about 5.09%. When a central bank leans against hot data, rate differentials stop widening and the dollar loses its engine.

The regime is not broken, but it is under test. The US threshold is a 10-year below 5.00% with DXY below 100.00. The yield half is 9bp away; the dollar half is about 1.6% away. Next week is the last window for Fed speakers before the blackout. If they confirm patience, the 5.00% line becomes the week’s key level.

October hike odds are near 27–34%, down from about 50% after CPI. Hammack warned the Fed may need to act more aggressively if an inflationary mindset sets in; she is the outlier this week. Blackout starts Saturday Oct 24.

ECB · depo 2.50% · next Oct 28–29

Lagarde and Knot leaned modestly hawkish. Lagarde said the ECB cannot wait for second-round effects to become fully visible. Still no backstop signal for French spreads.

BoE · 3.75% · next Nov 5

Markets price a high chance of a November hike. Bailey called above-target inflation “enormously frustrating” while noting second-round effects are subdued so far. That explains sterling’s 0.75% weekly gain.

BoJ · 1.25%

Board member Koeda said the BoJ should not be overly cautious about hiking. She placed neutral well above 1%. USD/JPY rejected 158.95 on Wednesday and is back below 158.

RBA · 4.60%

September employment came in weak. AUD/USD fell to 0.6894 on Thursday and is down 0.33% on the week, the worst major against the dollar. AUD/NZD lost 1.35%.

RBNZ 2.75% · BoC 2.25% · SNB 0.00%

RBNZ chief economist Conway said the late-October decision is open but a rise is more likely. That made NZD the best major on the week, +1.03%. Canada CPI on Monday is the next CAD input.

Analyst Conclusion
Dominant theme, primary risk, clearest setups

The dominant theme is a Fed that declined to chase hot data, and a dollar that lost its engine as a result. CPI, PPI and retail sales all beat. Fed speakers leaned against an October move, the 10-year fell to about 5.09% and DXY is heading for its first weekly fall in five, at 101.67.

The primary risk is a 10-year break below 5.00%. That is half of the regime-shift threshold, and next week is the last window for Fed speakers before the blackout. Over the weekend, Hormuz attacks and a possible easing of US sanctions on shadow-fleet tankers can move oil in opposite directions.

Both shorts paid their first targets and are now closed. EUR/USD was stopped at breakeven on the reversal; NZD/USD was closed at 0.5670 ahead of its technical invalidation as the dollar leg of the thesis turned. The GBP/USD squeeze signal is void after its named invalidation printed; the AUD/USD signal is carried but not triggered. EUR/GBP remains pending and under review.
